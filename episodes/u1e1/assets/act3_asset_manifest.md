## Beat 3 — Iroquois Confederacy / longhouse

### 3a. (RECOMMENDED) Reconstructed 15th-century Iroquoian longhouse
- URL: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/eb/Iroquoian_Village%2C_Ontario%2C_Canada28.JPG
- Source: Wikimedia Commons
- License: CC BY-SA 3.0 (credit photographer per file page)
- Depicts: "Turtle Clan Longhouse" — full-size reconstruction of a 15th-century Iroquoian longhouse, rebuilt on its original village site in Ontario, 4000×3000
- Period represented: 15th century (pre-contact) — the right era for the Confederacy's formation
- Anachronism: modern reconstruction — caption should say "reconstruction." Bark longhouses of this form are well documented for Haudenosaunee/Iroquoian peoples.

### 3b. Hochelaga village plan, 1556 (alt — village context)
- URL: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/La_Terra_De_Hochelaga_Nella_Nova_Francia%2C_Contributed_by_McGill_University_Library.jpg
- Source: McGill University Library; Giacomo Gastaldi's plan in Ramusio's *Delle Navigationi* (1556)
- License: Public domain
- Depicts: Plan of Hochelaga, the fortified Iroquoian village Jacques Cartier visited in 1535 — palisade, ~50 longhouses
- Period: 1535 (observed) / 1556 (published)
- Anachronism: St. Lawrence Iroquoians were related to but distinct from the Five Nations Haudenosaunee; do not label it a "Haudenosaunee village" without qualification.
