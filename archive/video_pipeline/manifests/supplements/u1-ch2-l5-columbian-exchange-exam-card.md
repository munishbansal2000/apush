# Exam Card — The Exchange, Both Ways

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The exchange (positions)

- **EASTWARD** — Maize, potato, sweet potato, tomato, cacao, tobacco. Even
  disease traveled east: sailors may even have carried syphilis back to Europe.
- **WESTWARD** — Wheat, sugarcane, bananas. Horses, pigs, cattle, goats,
  chickens. And the deadliest cargo: smallpox, measles, influenza — epidemic
  diseases that killed because Native peoples had no resistance.
- **MECHANISM** — A field of potatoes fed three times as many people as a
  field of wheat, and grew where wheat could not. More food, more people: a
  population boom built on American calories.
- **VERDICT** — The same ships carried both. Afro-Eurasia got the calories;
  the Americas got the germs. Growth on one side, catastrophe on the other.

## At stake

**AP Topic 1.4** — why contact produced growth and catastrophe at once: the
two sides brought different biology to the exchange, so the same ships
produced unequal results.

## Outcome

American crops reshape Afro-Eurasian diets and feed population growth after
1500; European epidemic diseases devastate Native populations (disease, not
warfare, is the main cause); the horse transforms Plains cultures.

## Dramatization note

The chronicler voice is dramatized (a Spanish observer's plausible letter
home), not a verbatim quote. Sugar engraving: *Nova Reperta* plate 13
(Saccharum), designed by Jan van der Straet, engraved by Philips Galle,
c. 1591–1600. Maize image: *De historia stirpium*, 1542 — Europe's first
printed image of an American plant.
