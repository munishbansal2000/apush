## Systematic findings (for parent / validator)

1. **Difficulty inflation (headline finding).** My blind judgments came in one notch
   below the author's tag on 11 of 13 items, all in the same direction. Six sampled
   items (#2, #6, #8, #9, #10, #13) are easy by CB norms: one-step recall with
   blatantly false distractors (absolute-worded or factually absurd options). Five more
   (#3, #4, #5, #7, #12) are medium: one-to-two-step reasoning with at most one
   plausible misconception distractor. None of the 13 required the multi-step reasoning
   or strong misconception traps that justify "hard." Consistent with this, the file
   distributions show **zero easy items** (u3: 30 medium/15 hard; u4: 26 medium/14 hard;
   u5: 26 medium/14 hard). Recommend recalibrating the difficulty rubric and/or the
   validator's difficulty-sanity rule; a bank with no easy items will skew any
   adaptive or tiered use.

2. **u4 topic_code scheme vs official CED numbering.** The u4 file uses topic codes
   4.1–4.14 (14 codes), while the official CB CED Period 4 has 13 topics (4.1–4.13),
   and the author's numbering appears shifted by one from ~4.5 onward relative to the
   official scheme (author's 4.7 = Expanding Democracy content, official 4.6; author's
   4.11 = An Age of Reform, official 4.10). This conflicts with the standing rule that
   topic codes must be real CB codes, not local inventions. Caveat: this is recalled
   from training knowledge of the CED, not verified against the live document — the
   parent should confirm against the actual Fall 2026 CED. Within the author's own
   scheme, the sampled u4 tags are internally consistent.

3. **Reasoning-process fit on Sourcing/Evidence items.** Items testing source situation
   (#7) or audience (#10), and "which evidence best supports this claim" items
   (#11, #13), map awkwardly onto Causation/Comparison/Continuity & Change. The
   mismatches here are mostly either-way calls, but #11 and #13 are the clearest case:
   a comparative claim about which section benefited calls for Comparison, not Causation.

4. **Borderline topic calls, author's tag defensible:** #3 (Shays: 3.7 vs 3.8),
   #10 (ctx5-03: 5.1 vs 5.2). Not errors — recorded as mismatches against my blind
   read for the tally, but either tag is justifiable.
