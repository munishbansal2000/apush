Day 98

“What is needed in Cuba at present is firm but liberal government of the people, for the people and by the people, under American military supervision, for the time being. … It must also be fully realized by those in charge that the Army of the United States is not in Cuba for the purpose of suppressing civil law and civil rights; but on the contrary, for the purpose of restoring and establishing civil law; that it stands inactive so long as the civil officers are able to execute the laws, and interferes only when they are unable to do so.”

—Major-General Leonard Wood, Military Governor of Santiago de Cuba, “The Existing Conditions and Needs in Cuba,” The North American Review, May 1899

Leonard Wood was Theodore Roosevelt’s partner in creating the 1st Volunteer Cavalry, the famous “Rough Riders.” During the Spanish-American War he was rapidly promoted, and after the conflict he became the military governor of Cuba. What is Wood’s perspective on his role as military governor? What insight does this give us into American imperial attitudes?
