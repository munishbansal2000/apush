Day 85

On July 12, 1893, a young scholar named Frederick Jackson Turner gave an address to a meeting of the American Historical Association at the World Columbian Exposition in Chicago. His talk was entitled “The Significance of the Frontier in American History.” Here Turner laid the foundation for his famous “Frontier Thesis,” which argued that the frontier experience was the most important force shaping American life. He wrote, “The frontier is the line of most rapid and effective Americanization. American social development has been continually beginning over again on the frontier. This perennial rebirth, this fluidity of American life, this expansion westward with its new opportunities, its continuous touch with the simplicity of primitive society, furnish the forces dominating American character . …” Turner’s thesis made him one of the most influential historians in American history.
