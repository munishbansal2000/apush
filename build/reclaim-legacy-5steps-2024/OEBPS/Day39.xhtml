Day 39

“Clear. mrs Springer grew very ill about 1 O Clok ys morn. Calld her women and was Safe Deliverd at 11 of a fine Son. I left her comfortable & returnd at 3 pm. mrs Bisbee & Lidia Came home with me. mrs Voce here Bakeing. old mr Cowen & wife & Son Drank Tea here. Georg Couch Departed this Life last wednsday Eving.”

—Martha Ballard, Diary, June 8, 1787

Martha Ballard was a midwife who lived in Maine. At the age of 50, she began a diary, which she maintained for 27 years until her death. In this diary, Ballard kept a brief record of her daily activities, including visits to women in childbirth and ailing neighbors, and also the comings and goings of family members, friends, and neighbors. The historian Laurel Thatcher Ulrich used the diary to write a biography of Ballard entitled A Midwife’s Tale (1990) that was awarded the Pulitzer Prize and several other awards. Clearly a source like Martha Ballard’s diary is not as easy to read as the public papers and memoirs left by important leaders. Why are sources like this diary important?
