Day 144

In the 1960s a new generation of “revisionist” historians questioned cold war orthodoxy and argued against the belief that the Soviet Union was solely responsible for the cold war, instead contending that the United States, pursuing expansive strategic and economic interests, made the dominant contribution to the onset of the struggle. A later generation of “post-revisionist” scholars challenged this interpretation and said that while both superpowers contributed to the coming of the cold war, the “revisionists” downplayed the effects of Soviet policies in precipitating conflict.

Why did the disputes between historians of the cold war take on a greater significance than the usual academic debates over the past?
