## Essay

The First Great Awakening transformed American identity by changing religion in the colonies. Before the Awakening, churches were boring to many people, and after it, religion became more emotional and personal, so colonial identity changed. [Thesis +1: minimal but defensible — it takes a position on "how far" (religion changed) rather than restating the prompt.]

George Whitefield was a preacher who traveled around the colonies giving sermons to large crowds of people. He told people they needed to have a personal conversion experience. Because of preachers like him, more colonists became interested in religion. The Awakening changed things but some things stayed the same too. [Evidence +0 (identification): only one specific example — Whitefield. The rubric needs two or more specific examples; "preachers like him" is not a second example.] [Evidence +0 (support): the single example is described, not tied to an argument about identity.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (continuity-and-change frame): present in its simplest form — "before... after... changed things but some things stayed the same." The frame structures the essay even though the content is thin.]

[Contextualization 0: no broader context — nothing about the established churches, the colonies' separateness, or why a shared religious experience would matter to identity.] [Complexity 0: "some things stayed the same" gestures at continuity but never says *what* stayed the same or why the limit matters — it is a placeholder, not a qualification.]
