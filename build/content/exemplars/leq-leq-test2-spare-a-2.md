## The essay

Massachusetts Bay and Virginia were very different colonies in early America. [Thesis +1: thin but historically defensible — it commits to a clear evaluative claim ("very different") about the extent of difference, establishing a basic line of reasoning, rather than restating the prompt.]

[Contextualization 0: the essay opens directly on the two colonies with no broader historical context — nothing about English colonization patterns, the religious conflicts behind Puritan migration, or the Atlantic economy to explain the conditions the colonies arose from.]

Virginia was about making money. It was founded at Jamestown in 1607, and the colonists grew tobacco to sell. Massachusetts Bay was about religion. The Puritans went there to practice their religion freely, and they built towns with churches. [Evidence 0 (identified): only ONE specific example appears — Jamestown (1607). Tobacco is named but with no person, date, or detail; the Puritans and their towns are described entirely in general terms. Two or more specific examples are required for this point.] [Evidence 0 (support): with only one identified example and no explanation of how any detail demonstrates the difference, the support point cannot be earned.]

The two colonies were also different in who lived there. Virginia had mostly men who came to get rich, while Massachusetts Bay had families. Comparing them shows that the reason a colony was founded decided what kind of society it became. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: the essay attempts comparison to frame its argument — weighing the two colonies against each other and offering founding purpose as the cause of the difference — even though the comparison rests on generalities rather than specifics.]

[Complexity 0: no qualification or counterevidence. The essay never considers what the colonies shared — English law, English identity, displacement of Native peoples — or any limit to the difference it claims.]
