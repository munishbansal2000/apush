## Essay

The Supreme Court affected Progressive regulation of corporations between 1885 and 1920. [Thesis +1, at the floor: "affected" is about as thin as a claim gets, but it is defensible and it responds to the prompt — so it earns. It gives no line of reasoning and no judgment of extent, which is the prompt's actual question.]

In Lochner v. New York, the Court said that a law limiting bakers' hours was "an illegal interference with the rights of individuals, both employers and employees, to make contracts regarding labor upon such terms as they may think best" (Doc. 4). This shows the Court stopped a labor law. [Evidence 0: quoted, not used — and one of only two documents in the essay. The quote is restated ("stopped a labor law") without any explanation of *why* the liberty-of-contract doctrine mattered beyond this one case, and two quoted documents cannot reach the three-document minimum for describing content.]

In U.S. v. E. C. Knight Co., the Court said the Sugar Trust's actions "bore no direct relation to commerce between states or with foreign countries" (Doc. 2). This shows the Court would not break up the trust. The Sherman Antitrust Act was passed in 1890 to stop trusts and monopolies, but the Court made it hard to use against them. [Evidence beyond documents +1: the Sherman Act's 1890 passage — a specific, relevant outside fact, and the essay's strongest sentence. One concrete fact, tied to the argument, is all this point requires.]

In conclusion, the Supreme Court affected Progressive regulation of corporations, sometimes stopping it.

[Contextualization 0: the essay never places the cases in a larger story — nothing about industrialization, the trust problem, or the Progressive movement. The documents are left to explain themselves, which they do not.]

[Sourcing 0 and Complexity 0: the essay never asks why the justices reasoned as they did, and it never weighs advance against inhibit — even though the prompt asks "to what extent" precisely so the student will.]
