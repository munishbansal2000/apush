# LEQ Exemplar — leq-drill-main (test-08) — target score 2/6

**Prompt:** Assess how far the abolitionist movement fostered change in America between 1830 and 1860.
**Period:** U4 · **Themes:** SOC, PCE · **Reasoning:** Causation
**Score earned: 2/6** — Thesis 1 · Contextualization 0 · Evidence 0 · Analysis & Reasoning 1

---

## Essay

The abolitionist movement fostered change in America between 1830 and 1860 because abolitionists worked to end slavery. They gave speeches and wrote newspapers against slavery, which caused more northerners to oppose it. [Thesis +1: minimal but defensible — it takes a position on the prompt's "how far" (it fostered change) instead of restating the question.]

The Underground Railroad was a network that helped enslaved people escape from the South to the North. Because it existed, some enslaved people were able to reach freedom, which showed that abolitionists were taking real action and not just talking. This caused tensions between the North and South to grow because southerners were angry about losing enslaved workers. [Evidence +0 (identification): only one specific example — the Underground Railroad. "Speeches and newspapers" in the introduction are too vague to count, and the rubric requires two or more specific examples.] [Evidence +0 (support): the single example is described but never developed into support for an argument about the *extent* of change.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (causation frame): the essay consistently explains through cause and effect — "because... which caused... this caused tensions to grow." Simple, but it is genuinely the causation frame structuring the argument.]

[Contextualization 0: nothing places abolitionism in a broader setting — no Second Great Awakening, no earlier gradualism or colonization movement, no explanation of why immediatism emerged in the 1830s specifically.] [Complexity 0: no qualification or tension — the essay claims change and never asks how much, where, or for whom.]

---

## Why this is a 2, not a 4

An honest two points: a position and because-language. The path upward is visible and short: (1) *context* — two sentences on what made the 1830s different (the Second Great Awakening's moral urgency, the break from gradual emancipation); (2) *a second specific example* — Garrison's *Liberator* or Douglass's *Narrative*, each with one sentence tying it to the thesis. Students writing at this level often believe they need "better vocabulary"; what they actually need is one more fact and one more "because."
