## Essay

The United States can be described as imperialist from 1890 to 1945 because during this period it took control of overseas territories and ruled over millions of people who were not American citizens. [Thesis +1: a defensible claim answering the prompt, with a line of reasoning — seizing territory and ruling non-citizens is what makes the label fit.]

In the late 1800s, the great European powers were dividing up Africa and Asia into colonies, and many Americans believed the United States needed colonies of its own to remain a great power and to find new markets for its industries. This competition with Europe helps explain why the United States began expanding overseas in the 1890s. [Contextualization +1: broader background — the European scramble and the search for markets — relevant to the prompt.]

The evidence that America built an empire is clear in the numbers. According to the 1940 census, the United States ruled 16,356,000 people in the Philippines and another 2,477,023 across Puerto Rico, Hawaii, Alaska, the Panama Canal Zone, Guam, the Virgin Islands, and American Samoa — more than 18 million colonial subjects in total, up sharply since 1920 (Doc. 6). A country that governs 18 million people outside its own borders is running an empire, whatever it calls it.

Americans at the time celebrated this empire openly. A 1914 Chicago Tribune cartoon showed figures representing the Philippines, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Cuba, and Panama being lifted from oppression into respectable suits, holding papers labeled "Filipino Assembly," "Education," and "Self Govt." (Doc. 4). An 1899 magazine cartoon showed President McKinley digging at Central America while Uncle Sam urged him to "finish the canal... and make our national expansion complete," with American flags flying over the Philippines, Hawaii, Cuba, and Puerto Rico (Doc. 7). These images show that expansion was a popular national project, not something done in secret.

The ideology behind the empire was paternalistic. An American colonial official wrote that Filipinos were "intelligent and polite" and "ambitious to improve their condition," but that they needed "proper care and direction" to become "a strength to our nation" (Doc. 5). This language of guardianship reveals the imperial mindset: other peoples were treated as children who needed American supervision rather than as equals entitled to independence.

The Spanish-American War of 1898 gave the United States the Philippines, Puerto Rico, and Guam, turning America into a colonial power almost overnight. [Evidence beyond documents +1: a specific outside fact — the 1898 war and what it transferred — supporting the argument.]

In conclusion, the American state was imperialist between 1890 and 1945: it conquered overseas territories, ruled millions of colonial subjects, and justified its rule as benevolent uplift.
