## Essay

The role of the federal government changed a lot between 1830 and 1910. In 1830 the government did not do very much, but by 1910 it was regulating businesses and passing reform laws, so its role had grown much bigger over time. [Thesis +1: minimal but defensible — it answers "how far" (a lot) with a comparative claim about change, rather than restating the prompt.]

One example is the Interstate Commerce Act of 1887, which was passed to regulate the railroads. Before this law, the federal government had not regulated businesses in this way. After it passed, the government had the power to control railroad rates, which was a big change from 1830. The government kept expanding its role after that with more laws. [Evidence +0 (identification): only one specific example — the Interstate Commerce Act. "More laws" is not a second example, and the rubric requires two or more specific ones.] [Evidence +0 (support): the single example is described accurately but never connected to an argument about the *extent* of change across the whole 1830–1910 period.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (continuity-and-change frame): present in basic form — "before this law... after it passed... a big change from 1830." The essay genuinely organizes around change over time.]

[Contextualization 0: no broader context — nothing about Jacksonian limited government, the Civil War as the hinge of expansion, or industrialization as the pressure behind regulation. The 1830 baseline is asserted ("did not do very much") but never established.] [Complexity 0: no qualification — no limits, no retreat on civil rights, no sense that the change was uneven.]
