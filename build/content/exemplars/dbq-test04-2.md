# DBQ Exemplar — test-04 (princeton-25e/dbq-drill): Fears of Communism, 1940–1959

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which fears of communism affected American society from 1940 to 1959.

**Target score: 2/7.** (Bracketed notes are teacher margin annotations, not student text.)

Fears of communism affected American society a great deal between 1940 and 1959. [Thesis +1: a weak thesis — it restates the prompt as a flat claim with no line of reasoning about extent — but it is a historically defensible claim that answers the question, so it earns the point.] A decoded KGB telegram from 1944 shows a Soviet agent in New York reporting that he recruited people to photograph materials for the atomic energy project (Doc 1). Senator Joseph McCarthy said in 1950 that he had a list of 205 communists working in the State Department (Doc 6). A 1955 advertisement in Civil Defense magazine sold metal identification tags for schoolchildren to wear during atomic-bomb evacuation drills so parents could identify them after an attack (Doc 4). So Americans were afraid of communists spying, afraid of communists in the government, and afraid of atomic bombs, which shows the fear affected society a lot. [Evidence +1 (first point): the content of three documents is accurately described. Second point 0: only three documents are used, and they are summarized in sequence rather than used to build an argument about extent.]
