## Essay

Immigration was debated in both time periods, 1754–1800 and 1875–1925, and people had different opinions about it. The debate changed somewhat between the two periods. [Thesis +1, barely: "people had different opinions" and "changed somewhat" is a defensible claim that answers the prompt, but it gives the reader almost nothing to follow. A thesis needs to say *how* things changed — this one only says that they did.]

Benjamin Franklin wrote, "Why should the [Germans] be suffered to swarm into our Settlements, and by herding together establish their Language and Manners to the Exclusion of ours?" (Doc. 1). This shows that Franklin did not like German immigrants. [Evidence 0: the document is quoted and restated, not used. Restating a quote ("did not like German immigrants") shows no understanding of *why* Franklin's cultural anxiety matters to the comparison the prompt asks for. Two quoted documents also fall short of the three-document minimum.]

Madison Grant wrote that the new immigrants were "the weak, the broken, and the mentally crippled of all races" (Doc. 6). This shows that Grant thought the new immigrants were bad. The Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882 banned Chinese immigrants from coming to America, which was a strict law. [Evidence beyond documents +1: the 1882 act is a specific, accurate outside fact relevant to restriction in the later period — the essay's strongest sentence.]

In conclusion, immigration was debated in both periods and the debate changed somewhat over time.

[Contextualization 0: the essay never places either debate in a larger story — no mention of the new republic's fragility, industrialization, or anything else beyond the documents. The reader gets no sense of *why* these debates happened when they did.]

[Sourcing 0 and Complexity 0: the essay never asks why Franklin or Grant wrote as they did, and it never weighs how much changed versus what stayed the same — even though "evaluate the extent" is the core task of the prompt.]
