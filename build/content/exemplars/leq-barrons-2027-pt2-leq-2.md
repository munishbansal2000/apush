**Test:** test-02 · **LEQ id:** barrons-2027-pt2-leq
**Prompt:** From the French and Indian War through the Civil War, Britain's North American colonies and then the United States fought a series of military conflicts. Explain how participation in these conflicts influenced American national identity in the period from 1754 to 1877.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine struggling-student work — a thin-but-defensible thesis, one specific example, and general causal language, but no broader context and no developed evidence. ## The essay

Fighting in wars from 1754 to 1877 helped Americans stop thinking of themselves as British and start thinking of themselves as Americans with their own national identity. [Thesis +1: thin but historically defensible — it commits to a claim about the direction of change (British → American identity) and establishes a basic line of reasoning across the period, rather than restating the prompt.]

[Contextualization 0: the essay opens directly on the topic with no broader historical context — nothing about the Anglo-French imperial struggle, British war debt, or Enlightenment ideas to explain the conditions behind the change.]

The French and Indian War was important because the colonists fought in it and then Britain started taxing them. The Stamp Act of 1765 made the colonists angry because they had to pay taxes on paper and documents. This made them feel less British. [Evidence 0 (identified): only ONE specific example appears — the Stamp Act of 1765. The French and Indian War itself is discussed only in general terms. Two or more specific examples are required for this point.] [Evidence 0 (support): the single example is described ("made the colonists angry") but never connected to an argument about identity beyond the bare assertion "made them feel less British" — with only one identified example and no developed explanation, the support point cannot be earned.]

Later wars continued this change. The American Revolution made the colonies independent, and the Civil War ended slavery, which changed what America meant. Wars caused Americans to unite together against common enemies, and this unity is what built national identity over time. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: the essay uses causation and change-over-time to frame its argument — wars caused unity, and unity built identity across the period — even though the reasoning is general and unsupported by specifics.]

[Complexity 0: no qualification, no acknowledgment of counterevidence. The essay never considers that many colonists remained Loyalists, that wars like the Mexican-American War divided Americans, or that the identity being forged excluded many people.]
