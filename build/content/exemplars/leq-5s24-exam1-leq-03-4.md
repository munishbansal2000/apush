# LEQ Exemplar — Target Score: 4/6

**Test:** test-04 · **LEQ id:** 5s24-exam1-leq-03
**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which late nineteenth century urbanization affected politics, immigration, and popular culture.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine mid-level student work — a real thesis, real context, real examples, but the examples are listed rather than developed and the argument is never complicated. Margin annotations in [brackets] mark each rubric row earned or lost, with the specific reason. A scoring tally follows the essay.

## The essay

In the late 1800s, American industry grew rapidly with steel, railroads, and factories, which created jobs in cities. At the same time, millions of immigrants arrived from Southern and Eastern Europe looking for work. [Contextualization +1: industrialization and the new immigration wave are broader developments beyond urbanization itself that explain why cities grew.]

Late nineteenth-century urbanization greatly affected American politics, immigration, and popular culture by changing how cities were governed, where immigrants lived, and how people spent their free time. [Thesis +1: a historically defensible claim about extent ("greatly affected") that establishes a line of reasoning across all three prompt categories.]

Urbanization affected politics through machine politics. Boss Tweed ran Tammany Hall in New York City. The machine gave jobs to immigrants in exchange for their votes. Reformers did not like the machines because they were corrupt. [Evidence +1 (identified): specifics named — Boss Tweed, Tammany Hall, the jobs-for-votes exchange. Evidence (support) 0: the paragraph never explains *how* this shows urbanization affecting politics beyond the bare facts. The jobs-for-votes mechanism is stated but its significance — incorporating immigrants into politics, the tension between democracy and corruption — is never drawn out. The examples are listed, not used.]

Urbanization affected immigration because immigrants moved into cities and formed ethnic neighborhoods. There were Italian, Jewish, Irish, and Polish neighborhoods with their own churches and newspapers. Jane Addams founded Hull House in Chicago in 1889 to help immigrants. Jacob Riis wrote *How the Other Half Lives* in 1890 about tenement poverty. [More specifics identified — ethnic neighborhoods, Hull House (1889), Riis (1890) — but each gets one sentence and none is connected to an argument. What did the neighborhoods *do*? What did Hull House *change*? The paragraph is a catalog.]

Urbanization affected popular culture by creating new forms of entertainment. Vaudeville theaters, Coney Island amusement parks, department stores, and professional baseball teams gave city workers things to do in their free time. [Specifics named — vaudeville, Coney Island, department stores, baseball — but with no explanation of their cultural effect: no mention of shared experience, class mixing, or what mass leisure meant for a city of strangers. Support point remains unearned.]

These changes happened because industrial jobs drew people to cities, and the concentration of people in cities created both problems and new opportunities. Urbanization caused political machines to rise, immigrant communities to form, and mass entertainment to develop. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: causation frames the argument — industrial jobs caused migration, density caused machines, neighborhoods, and entertainment.]

[Complexity 0: the essay never qualifies or complicates its claim. No mention that machines were both democratic and corrupt, that immigration policy excluded some groups (Chinese Exclusion Act), that amusements were segregated, or that most Americans still lived outside cities — the argument runs in one direction throughout.]
