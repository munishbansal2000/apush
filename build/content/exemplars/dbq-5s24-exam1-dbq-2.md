## Essay

The United States did a lot of things in Latin America between 1899 and 1917, and those things affected Latin American countries in different ways. [Thesis +1, but only just: this is about as thin as a thesis can get while still earning the point. It makes a claim ("affected Latin American countries") and gestures at a line of reasoning ("in different ways"), and it is historically defensible. A thesis this vague leaves the reader with no roadmap — and in a stronger essay it would be sharpened into a real argument.]

President Roosevelt said that "chronic wrongdoing, or an impotence which results in a general loosening of the ties of civilized society, may... ultimately require intervention by some civilized nation" (Doc. 3). This shows that Roosevelt wanted the United States to intervene in Latin America. [Evidence 0: the document is quoted, not used. Quoting a line and adding "this shows..." does not demonstrate understanding of the document's content — and with only two documents quoted, the essay falls short of the three-document minimum anyway. A point the rubric rewards: *describing* what a document says in the student's own words, tied to the argument.]

President Taft talked about "substituting dollars for bullets" (Doc. 5). This shows that Taft wanted to use money instead of fighting. The Panama Canal was built during this time and it opened in 1914, which made the United States more powerful in Latin America. [Evidence beyond documents +1: the canal opening is a specific, accurate fact outside the documents, and it connects to the argument about American power. This is the essay's best move — one memorized fact, placed where it supports the point.]

In conclusion, the United States affected Latin America in different ways between 1899 and 1917, like with intervention and money.

[Contextualization 0: there is no broader background anywhere in this essay — nothing about the Spanish-American War, industrialization, or the Monroe Doctrine. The prompt's period does not explain itself; one or two sentences placing these events in a larger story would earn this point.]

[Sourcing 0 and Complexity 0: the essay never considers why any author wrote what they wrote, and it never qualifies its vague claim. Both points require the student to step back from the documents and think — about perspective, and about limits or contradictions in the argument.]
