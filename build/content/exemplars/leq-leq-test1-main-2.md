# LEQ Exemplar — Target Score: 2/6

**Test:** test-05 · **LEQ id:** leq-test1-main
**Prompt:** Assess how far social movements reshaped American society between 1945 and 1980.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine struggling-student work — a thin-but-defensible thesis, one specific example, and general causal language, but no broader context and no developed evidence. Margin annotations in [brackets] mark each rubric row earned or lost, with the specific reason. A scoring tally follows the essay.

## The essay

Social movements changed American society a lot between 1945 and 1980 by fighting for civil rights and women's rights. [Thesis +1: thin but historically defensible — it commits to a claim about extent ("a lot") and establishes a basic line of reasoning by naming two areas of change, rather than restating the prompt.]

[Contextualization 0: the essay opens directly on the movements with no broader historical context — nothing about postwar prosperity, the Cold War, or WWII's dislocations to explain the conditions the movements arose from.]

The civil rights movement was the most important. Black Americans protested against segregation in the South, and Martin Luther King Jr. led marches and gave speeches. The Civil Rights Act of 1964 made segregation illegal. This changed the South because Black people could now use the same public places as white people. [Evidence 0 (identified): only ONE specific example appears — the Civil Rights Act of 1964. King is named but with no specific march, speech, or event; the protests are described entirely in general terms. Two or more specific examples are required for this point.] [Evidence 0 (support): the single example is stated but its significance is asserted in one clause ("changed the South because…") without any developed explanation of how it reshaped society beyond the immediate effect — named, not used in an argument.]

The women's movement also changed things by pushing for equal rights for women in jobs and education. When people protest and organize, it forces the government to pay attention and make new laws, which is how social movements reshaped society during this period. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: the essay uses causation to frame its argument — protest and organization caused government action — even though the reasoning is general and the women's movement paragraph contains no specifics at all.]

[Complexity 0: no qualification or counterevidence. The essay never considers backlash, incomplete victories, or anything the movements failed to change — the argument runs in one direction throughout.]
