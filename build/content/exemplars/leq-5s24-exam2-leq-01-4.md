# LEQ Exemplar — Target Score: 4/6

**Test:** test-01 · **LEQ id:** 5s24-exam2-leq-01
**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which reform movements played an important role in shaping American society from 1820 to 1860.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine mid-level student work — a real attempt with a real thesis, real context, and real examples, but the examples are listed rather than developed and the argument is never complicated. Margin annotations in [brackets] mark each rubric row earned or lost, with the specific reason. A scoring tally follows the essay.

## The essay

The market revolution changed how Americans worked and lived in the early 1800s, with factories and canals pulling people into new kinds of jobs and towns. [Contextualization +1: the market revolution is a broader development beyond the reform topic that sets up why reform arose.]

Reform movements played an important role in shaping American society from 1820 to 1860 because they challenged slavery, pushed for women's rights, and fought against alcohol abuse. [Thesis +1: a historically defensible claim that establishes a line of reasoning — three named areas of impact — rather than merely restating the prompt.]

Abolitionism was one major reform. William Lloyd Garrison published the *Liberator* starting in 1831. Frederick Douglass wrote his *Narrative* in 1845. The Underground Railroad helped enslaved people escape to the North. These were all part of the antislavery movement. [Evidence +1 (identified): three specific examples are named — the *Liberator* (1831), the *Narrative* (1845), the Underground Railroad. Evidence (support) 0: the paragraph never says what any of them *did* for the argument. The *Liberator* is named but its effect is never explained; the *Narrative* gets one clause; the Underground Railroad gets one sentence. The examples are listed, not used — none is tied to the thesis claim about shaping society.]

Women's rights was another reform. The Seneca Falls Convention happened in 1848 and produced the Declaration of Sentiments. Temperance reformers formed the American Temperance Society in 1826, and Maine passed a prohibition law in 1851. Horace Mann promoted public schools in Massachusetts. [More specifics identified — Seneca Falls (1848), the American Temperance Society (1826), the Maine law (1851) — but each gets a single sentence and none is connected to the thesis. The paragraph reads as a catalog, not an argument. Support point remains unearned.]

These reforms show that Americans in this period wanted to improve society. The reforms happened because the Second Great Awakening made people feel personally responsible for fixing moral problems, and the market revolution gave them the organizations and newspapers to act on that feeling. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: causation frames the argument — religious revival and economic change caused reform's rise, and reform caused pressure for social change. The reasoning is present even though the evidence behind it is thin.]

[Complexity 0: the essay never qualifies, limits, or complicates its claim. There is no acknowledgment that reform was mostly Northern, that its victories were partial, or that abolition ultimately required war. The argument runs in one direction from start to finish.]

**Teaching note:** This is the most common 4/6 profile — the student *knows* the facts but treats the essay as a list. The fix is small and concrete: after naming each example, add one sentence beginning "This shows…" or "This mattered because…". That single habit converts listed evidence into supporting evidence.
