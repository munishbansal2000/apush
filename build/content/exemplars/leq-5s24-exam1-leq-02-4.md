## Essay

England founded its North American colonies largely for economic reasons, hoping to gain wealth through trade under the system of mercantilism. But the different regions had different climates and soil, so they developed different economies. The South grew tobacco and rice on plantations, New England relied on small farms, fishing, and trade, and the Middle colonies grew wheat and grain. Differing economies shaped differing social structures to a great extent: the South developed a hierarchical society based on plantation slavery, New England developed a society of towns and independent farmers, and the Middle colonies developed a diverse commercial society. [Thesis +1: defensible claim with a line of reasoning — three economies, three social orders.] [Contextualization +1: connects the colonies to the broader context of English mercantilist motives and the geographic differences behind the economic divergence.]

In the South, plantations growing tobacco in the Chesapeake and rice in South Carolina needed many workers, so planters used enslaved African labor. This created a society with wealthy planters at the top and enslaved people at the bottom. In New England, the rocky soil was bad for large-scale farming, so families worked small farms and towns held town meetings. There were fewer enslaved people in New England. In the Middle colonies, the fertile land supported wheat farming, and cities like Philadelphia became centers of trade. The Middle colonies were ethnically diverse, with Germans and Scots-Irish settlers. These differences in economies led to differences in social structures. [Evidence +1 (identification): plantation slavery in the South, town meetings in New England, Philadelphia trade and Middle-colony diversity — 2+ specific examples.] [Evidence +0 (support): the examples are described but never *used* comparatively. The paragraph never explains *how* the plantation economy produced the planter hierarchy (as opposed to merely coinciding with it), never contrasts *why* town meetings fit a farming economy, and never connects Philadelphia's commerce to its diversity — the comparison the thesis promises is asserted, not demonstrated.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (comparison frame): the essay does organize by region-to-region comparison throughout, which is the correct reasoning skill for the prompt.]

In conclusion, differing economies shaped differing social structures in the English colonies to a great extent, as shown by the plantation South, town-based New England, and diverse Middle colonies. [Complexity 0: no qualification anywhere — the essay never notes that slavery existed in all three regions, that English gender and legal norms crossed regional lines, or that any region varied internally. The conclusion restates the three-part thesis without weighing it.]

## Why this is a 4, not a 6

Everything the student knows is correct, and the regional structure is exactly right for a comparison prompt. The two missing points share one root cause: the essay *labels* differences but never *explains* them. Each paragraph needs one sentence of mechanism — "because tobacco required X, planters needed Y, which produced Z." And complexity is waiting in the facts the student already half-knows: if they had added "though slavery existed in New England too," the essay would have had to weigh its own claim — and that weighing is the point.
