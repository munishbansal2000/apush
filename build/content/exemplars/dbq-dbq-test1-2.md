## Essay

America's foreign policy changed between 1914 and 1917. [Thesis +1, at the floor: a single defensible claim with the barest line of reasoning. It answers the prompt, so it earns — but it tells the reader nothing about *how* or *how much* policy changed, which is the entire job of an "evaluate the extent" thesis.]

President Wilson said that Americans should act "in the true spirit of neutrality, which is the spirit of impartiality and fairness and friendliness to all concerned" (Doc. 1). This shows that America was neutral at the start. [Evidence 0: quoted, not used — and one of only two documents in the essay. The rubric needs three documents *described in the student's own words* and tied to the argument; a quote plus "this shows..." does neither.]

Later, Wilson said that if Germany kept using submarines against passenger ships, the United States would "sever diplomatic relations with the Government of the German Empire altogether" (Doc. 7). This shows that America was not neutral anymore. Germany sank the Lusitania in 1915, which made Americans angry at Germany. [Evidence beyond documents +1: the Lusitania sinking — a specific, relevant outside fact, and the essay's strongest move. Note how little it takes: one concrete fact, placed where it supports the point.]

In conclusion, America's foreign policy changed between 1914 and 1917 because of Germany's actions.

[Contextualization 0: nothing in this essay reaches beyond the documents — no mention of the tradition against European alliances, the immigrant population, or the wartime economy. One or two background sentences would earn this point outright.]

[Sourcing 0 and Complexity 0: the essay never considers why Wilson framed things as he did in either document, and it never qualifies its claim — for instance, by noting that American trade had favored the Allies from the start, which would complicate the simple neutral-to-not-neutral story.]
