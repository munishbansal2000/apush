# LEQ Exemplar — Target Score: 4/6

**Test:** test-02 · **LEQ id:** barrons-2027-pt2-leq
**Prompt:** From the French and Indian War through the Civil War, Britain's North American colonies and then the United States fought a series of military conflicts. Explain how participation in these conflicts influenced American national identity in the period from 1754 to 1877.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine mid-level student work — a real thesis, real context, real examples, but the examples are listed rather than developed and the argument is never complicated. Margin annotations in [brackets] mark each rubric row earned or lost, with the specific reason. A scoring tally follows the essay.

## The essay

In the 1700s Britain and France competed for control of North America, fighting several wars that ended with Britain's victory in the French and Indian War. Britain came out of the war with a huge debt, which is why Parliament started taxing the colonies. [Contextualization +1: the Anglo-French struggle and British war debt are broader context explaining the conditions for the identity change.]

Fighting in wars from 1754 to 1877 helped Americans develop their own national identity, changing from British subjects into citizens of the United States. [Thesis +1: a historically defensible claim establishing a line of reasoning — a transformation from British subjects to American citizens across the period.]

The French and Indian War was the first step. Colonists fought alongside the British army and gained military experience. After the war, Britain passed the Proclamation of 1763 and the Stamp Act of 1765. These laws angered the colonists. [Evidence +1 (identified): two specifics named — the Proclamation of 1763 and the Stamp Act of 1765. Evidence (support) 0: the paragraph never explains *how* these laws changed identity. "These laws angered the colonists" is a reaction, not an argument about identity — the examples are listed, not tied to the thesis.]

The American Revolution finished the break with Britain. The Declaration of Independence was written in 1776. The War of 1812 increased nationalism, and Francis Scott Key wrote the "Star-Spangled Banner" in 1814. The Era of Good Feelings followed. [More specifics identified — the Declaration (1776), the "Star-Spangled Banner" (1814), the Era of Good Feelings — but again each is named in a single clause with no explanation of what it did to identity. The paragraph is a timeline, not an argument.]

The Civil War was the final step in shaping identity. Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation in 1863 and gave the Gettysburg Address the same year. These events changed what the nation stood for. [Specifics named — 1863, Emancipation, Gettysburg — but "changed what the nation stood for" is asserted, not shown. No explanation of *how* emancipation reshaped identity. Support point remains unearned.]

Each war caused a change in how Americans saw themselves: the French and Indian War caused colonists to question British rule, the Revolution caused them to become independent, and the Civil War caused the nation to become unified around freedom. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: causation frames the argument — each conflict is presented as causing a stage of identity change across the period.]

[Complexity 0: the essay never qualifies or complicates the claim. No mention that Loyalists rejected the new identity, that the Mexican-American War divided the country over slavery, or that Reconstruction's promises were abandoned — the argument runs in one direction throughout.]
