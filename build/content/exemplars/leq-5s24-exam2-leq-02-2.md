# LEQ Exemplar — 5s24-exam2-leq-02 (test-06) — target score 2/6

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which differing ideas about the nature of government shaped the emergence of political parties in the 1780s and 1790s.
**Period:** U3 · **Themes:** PCE, NAT · **Reasoning:** Causation
**Score earned: 2/6** — Thesis 1 · Contextualization 0 · Evidence 0 · Analysis & Reasoning 1

---

## Essay

Differing ideas about the nature of government shaped the first political parties in America. Alexander Hamilton wanted a strong national government, and Thomas Jefferson wanted a weak one, and because they disagreed with each other, two political parties formed in the 1790s. [Thesis +1: a minimal but historically defensible claim that responds to the prompt. It states a position rather than restating the question, which is all the thesis point requires — though it establishes no real line of reasoning.]

Hamilton was Secretary of the Treasury under Washington. He wanted the government to create a national bank so the country's finances would be stronger. Jefferson thought the bank was unconstitutional because the Constitution did not specifically give the government that power. This disagreement caused Hamilton's supporters to become Federalists and Jefferson's supporters to become Democratic-Republicans. Later the parties also disagreed about other things. In the end, ideas about government were the reason the parties emerged. [Evidence +0 (identification): only one specific example is developed — the Bank debate. The rubric requires two or more specific examples for the identification point, and vague gestures ("other things") do not count.] [Evidence +0 (support): with only one example identified, there is nothing to tie to the argument at the level the rubric requires.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (causation frame): thin but present — the essay structures its explanation around causation throughout ("because they disagreed... this disagreement caused...").]

[Contextualization 0: the essay never places the parties in any broader context — no mention of the Revolution's aftermath, the Articles of Confederation, or the ratification debate that made the question of federal power urgent.] [Complexity 0: no qualification, contradiction, or weighing of causes — the essay presents one cause and stops.]

## Why this is a 2, not a 4

This is the classic "I know the story but not the history" essay. It earns its two points honestly: it takes a position, and it explains with because-language. To climb to a 4, it needs two things, both learnable: (1) *context* — one or two sentences at the start placing the 1790s inside a bigger story (the failed Articles, the fight over ratification); (2) *a second developed example* — the Jay Treaty split or the Alien and Sedition Acts, each with one sentence saying how it connects to the thesis. Complexity comes later; first the essay needs a foundation to complicate.
