## Essay

Differing economies shaped differing social structures in the English colonies. The South had plantations and slavery, while the North had small farms and towns, so the economies caused the societies to be different. [Thesis +1: minimal but defensible — it takes a position on "to what extent" (economies caused the differences) rather than restating the prompt.]

In the South, wealthy planters owned large tobacco plantations and used enslaved Africans to work the fields. This was different from New England, where families farmed their own land and there were fewer enslaved people. Because the South's economy depended on plantation crops, its society had a strict hierarchy with planters at the top, unlike the North. [Evidence +0 (identification): only one regional case is specifically developed — the southern plantation system. New England appears only as an undeveloped contrast ("small farms"), and the Middle colonies are absent entirely; the rubric requires two or more specific examples.] [Evidence +0 (support): the single example is described, not tied to an argument about *how* the economy produced the hierarchy.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (comparison frame): genuinely present — "while," "this was different from," "unlike the North" — the essay thinks comparatively throughout, even with thin material.]

[Contextualization 0: no broader context — nothing about why England founded the colonies, mercantilism, or the geographic differences that made the economies diverge in the first place.] [Complexity 0: the essay presents one cause and one pattern with no qualification — no shared institutions, no exceptions, no weighing.]

## Why this is a 2, not a 4

The student has the right instinct — compare regions, explain with because-language — and earns two points for it. What is missing is entirely additive: *context* (one sentence on English colonial motives and geography) and *the missing regions* (New England and especially the Middle colonies, each with one specific detail like town meetings or Philadelphia's mixed labor force). The comparison frame is already there; it just needs more to compare.
