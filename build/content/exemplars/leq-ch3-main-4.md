# LEQ Exemplar — leq-ch3-main (test-10) — target score 4/6

**Prompt:** Assess how far the role of the federal government changed between 1830 and 1910.
**Period:** U4/U6 · **Themes:** PCE · **Reasoning:** Continuity & Change
**Score earned: 4/6** — Thesis 1 · Contextualization 1 · Evidence 1 · Analysis & Reasoning 1

## Essay

In 1830, the federal government played a limited role in American life. President Andrew Jackson believed in small government and states' rights, as shown by his veto of the Maysville Road bill and his veto of the Bank of the United States. The federal government's main jobs were delivering mail, collecting tariffs, and handling foreign policy. Over the next eighty years, the country went through the Civil War, Reconstruction, industrialization, and the Progressive Era. The role of the federal government changed enormously between 1830 and 1910, growing from a limited government into an active one that regulated the economy, although some limits remained. [Thesis +1: defensible claim answering "how far," with a line of reasoning — growth into activism, with limits.] [Contextualization +1: establishes the 1830 baseline concretely (Jackson's vetoes, the short list of federal functions) and names the era-spanning forces — Civil War, industrialization, Progressivism — that will drive the change.]

During the Civil War, the federal government expanded its power. Congress passed the Homestead Act, the Morrill Act, and the Pacific Railway Act in 1862. The government also issued greenbacks and created a national banking system. After the war, the government passed the Interstate Commerce Act of 1887 and the Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890 to regulate railroads and trusts. In the Progressive Era, Theodore Roosevelt supported the Hepburn Act of 1906, the Pure Food and Drug Act, and conservation of natural resources. These actions show that the federal government's role changed a great deal. However, the Supreme Court sometimes limited the government, as in *Lochner v. New York*, and the government did little to protect the civil rights of African Americans after Reconstruction ended. [Evidence +1 (identification): the 1862 land laws, greenbacks, the Interstate Commerce and Sherman Acts, the Hepburn Act, Pure Food and Drug Act, *Lochner* — 2+ specific examples.] [Evidence +0 (support): the paragraph lists laws without developing what any of them *did* or how they prove the thesis. "The government also issued greenbacks" is never connected to the claim about a changed federal role; the Progressive laws are named, not explained; *Lochner* appears without any account of what it limited. The reader is given an inventory, not an argument.] [Analysis & Reasoning +1 (continuity-and-change frame): the essay consistently measures the later period against the 1830 baseline — "changed enormously... growing from a limited government into an active one" — organizing evidence as change over time.]

In conclusion, the federal government's role changed enormously between 1830 and 1910, though limits such as Supreme Court decisions and the retreat from civil rights remained. [Complexity 0: the conclusion restates the thesis. The material for complexity is sitting in the essay — a government expanding and retreating at once — but the writer never pauses on the tension. Two sentences on *why* the same era produced both regulation and retreat would earn the point.]

## Why this is a 4, not a 6

The historical knowledge here is strong — the law inventory is nearly complete and correctly dated. Both lost points come from the same missing habit: the "so what" sentence. Every named law needs one clause saying what it empowered the government to do that it could not do in 1830; every limit needs one clause on what it reveals. And complexity is not extra credit for extra facts — it is noticing that your own evidence pulls in two directions and saying so out loud.
