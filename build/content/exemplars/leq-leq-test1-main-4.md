**Test:** test-05 · **LEQ id:** leq-test1-main
**Prompt:** Assess how far social movements reshaped American society between 1945 and 1980.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine mid-level student work — a real thesis, real context, real examples, but the examples are listed rather than developed and the argument is never complicated. ## The essay

After World War II, the United States entered a period of economic prosperity, and the Cold War made American leaders claim the country stood for freedom around the world. At the same time, Black Americans who had served in the war came home to segregation. [Contextualization +1: postwar prosperity, the Cold War, and Black veterans' experience are broader developments beyond the movements that explain the conditions they arose from.]

Social movements reshaped American society a great deal between 1945 and 1980 by ending legal segregation, expanding women's rights, and challenging traditional cultural norms. [Thesis +1: a historically defensible claim about extent ("a great deal") that establishes a line of reasoning across three areas of change.]

The civil rights movement ended legal segregation in the South. The Montgomery bus boycott happened in 1955–56 after Rosa Parks was arrested. Martin Luther King Jr. became a national leader. The Civil Rights Act was passed in 1964 and the Voting Rights Act in 1965. [Evidence +1 (identified): specifics named — the Montgomery boycott (1955–56), Parks, the CRA (1964), the VRA (1965). Evidence (support) 0: the paragraph never explains *how* any of these reshaped society. The boycott is dated but its significance is never stated; the two landmark laws are named in a single sentence with no word on what they changed. The examples are listed, not used.]

The women's movement expanded women's rights. The National Organization for Women was founded in 1966. Title IX was passed in 1972. *Roe v. Wade* was decided in 1973. The gay rights movement began with the Stonewall uprising in 1969. [More specifics identified — NOW (1966), Title IX (1972), *Roe* (1973), Stonewall (1969) — but again each is a single clause with no explanation of effect. What did Title IX *do*? What did *Roe* *change*? The paragraph is a timeline, not an argument.]

These movements changed society because organized protest put pressure on the government, and the government responded with new laws. The civil rights movement caused Congress to pass civil rights laws, and the women's movement caused changes in education and reproductive rights. Later there was some backlash, with conservatives opposing busing and affirmative action. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: causation frames the argument — protest caused legislation — and the final sentence gestures at change over time (movement → backlash).]

[Complexity 0: the "backlash" sentence is a passing mention, not an argument — it names no specific (no Nixon, no Reagan, no *Bakke*, no ERA defeat) and never qualifies the thesis. The essay never seriously engages counterevidence or limits; the claim that movements "reshaped society a great deal" is never tested against what they failed to change.]

**Teaching note:** This essay is one sentence per paragraph from a 5. The student knows that Title IX and *Roe* matter but never says *how* — the classic gap between identifying evidence and supporting an argument. The repair: ban the "X happened in YEAR" sentence unless it is followed by "which changed…". Also note the common shortfall on display: mentioning backlash in passing does not earn the point. Complexity requires *arguing with* the counterevidence — specific limits, specific reversals — not nodding at it.
