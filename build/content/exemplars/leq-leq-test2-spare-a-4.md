# LEQ Exemplar — Target Score: 4/6

**Test:** test-03 · **LEQ id:** leq-test2-spare-a
**Prompt:** Assess how far the Massachusetts Bay Colony differed from the Virginia colony between 1607 and 1754.

**How to read this:** The essay below is written as genuine mid-level student work — a real thesis, real context, real examples, but the examples are listed rather than developed and the argument is never complicated. Margin annotations in [brackets] mark each rubric row earned or lost, with the specific reason. A scoring tally follows the essay.

## The essay

In the 1600s, religious conflict in England pushed many Puritans to leave, and in the 1630s thousands of them migrated to New England in the Great Migration. Virginia had been founded earlier by a joint-stock company looking for profit. [Contextualization +1: the religious upheavals behind the Great Migration and company-driven colonization are broader developments explaining why the colonies differed.]

Massachusetts Bay and Virginia differed a great deal from each other in their economies, societies, and governments between 1607 and 1754. [Thesis +1: a historically defensible claim that establishes a line of reasoning — three named axes of difference (economy, society, government).]

The economies were different. Virginia grew tobacco, which John Rolfe introduced in the 1610s, and used indentured servants and later enslaved Africans to work the plantations. Massachusetts Bay had small farms and did not grow a cash crop like tobacco. [Evidence +1 (identified): specifics named — Rolfe's tobacco (1610s), indentured servants, enslaved labor. Evidence (support) 0: the paragraph never explains *why* the economies differed (no mention of soil, the headright system, or how tobacco shaped the labor force) and never connects the economic facts to the argument about differing societies. The facts are listed, not used.]

The societies were different too. Massachusetts Bay was founded by Puritans, and John Winthrop gave his "city upon a hill" sermon in 1630. Families migrated together and built towns with churches. Virginia was founded at Jamestown in 1607, and most of the early migrants were single men. Harvard College was founded in 1636. [More specifics identified — Winthrop's sermon (1630), Jamestown (1607), Harvard (1636) — but each is a single clause with no explanation of what it shows. The paragraph reads as a set of facts placed side by side, not a comparison that argues anything.]

The governments also differed. Virginia created the House of Burgesses in 1619. Massachusetts Bay had town meetings where male church members could participate. [Specifics named — the Burgesses (1619), town meetings — but with no analysis of what the difference meant: who could participate, what powers each body had, or how either reflected the colony's social order. Support point remains unearned.]

The two colonies differed because they were founded for different reasons: Virginia was founded to make money, and Massachusetts Bay was founded for religious purposes. This caused their economies, societies, and governments to develop differently. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: comparison frames the argument — the essay is organized around weighing the two colonies against each other on three axes, with founding purpose offered as the cause of the differences.]

[Complexity 0: the essay never qualifies its claim. No mention that both colonies kept English legal traditions, that both displaced Native peoples, or that the differences had limits — the argument runs in one direction throughout.]

**Teaching note:** This is the classic comparison-essay failure mode: describing Colony A, then describing Colony B, without ever saying what the difference *means*. The repair is one sentence per paragraph of the form "This difference mattered because…". The town-meeting paragraph is the clearest opportunity — one sentence on who was included and who was not would start earning the support point.
