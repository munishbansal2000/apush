## The essay

From 1820 to 1860, reform movements played an important role in shaping American society because they challenged slavery and pushed for women's rights. [Thesis +1: thin but historically defensible — it commits to a claim about extent ("important role") and establishes a line of reasoning by naming two areas of impact, rather than restating the prompt word for word.]

[Contextualization 0: the essay jumps straight into the topic with no broader historical context. Nothing about the market revolution, the Second Great Awakening, or Jacksonian-era democratic expansion explains the conditions reform arose from.]

The most important reform was abolitionism. Slavery was a big problem in the South, and reformers in the North wanted to end it. People gave speeches and wrote against slavery, and this made more Northerners oppose it. The Seneca Falls Convention in 1848 was when women met to demand equal rights, which was an important step for women's rights. [Evidence 0 (identified): only ONE specific example appears — Seneca Falls (1848). Abolitionism is discussed entirely in general terms: no person, publication, organization, or event is named. Two or more specific examples are required for this point. Evidence 0 (support): with only one identified example, and that example (Seneca Falls) mentioned without any explanation of its effect on society, the support point cannot be earned either.]

Reform movements caused changes in society because when people organized and protested, it put pressure on the government and changed how people thought about moral issues. This is why reform was important in shaping America before the Civil War. [Analysis & Reasoning +1: the essay uses causation to frame its argument — organized reform activity caused shifts in public thinking and pressure on government — even though the reasoning is general and unsupported by specifics.]

[Complexity 0: no qualification, no acknowledgment of limits or counterevidence. The essay treats reform as uniformly successful and never considers what reform failed to change, where it was weak, or what contradicted its claims.]
