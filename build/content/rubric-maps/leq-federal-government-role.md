# Rubric Map — LEQ: The Role of the Federal Government, 1830–1910

**Source file:** `build/reclaim-merged/staged/princeton-25e/leq-ch3.json` (id: `leq-ch3-main`)
**Test:** test-10
**Prompt:** "Assess how far the role of the federal government changed between 1830 and 1910."
**Targeted reasoning:** Continuity & Change

**Rubric reminders:** 6 points — thesis (a defensible claim taking a position on *how far* the role changed, more than a restatement); contextualization (broader context: Jacksonian suspicion of concentrated power, the Civil War as the great expander of federal capacity, industrialization creating national problems states couldn't handle); evidence (at least 2 specific examples = 1 pt; used to support the argument = 1 pt); analysis & reasoning (continuity-and-change structures the argument = 1 pt; complexity = 1 pt).

## 1. Thesis framing

**Strong example thesis:** "The federal government's role changed enormously between 1830 and 1910 — from Jacksonian limited government to Civil War–era activism and Gilded Age economic regulation — yet the change was bounded: laissez-faire ideology, a conservative Supreme Court, and federal abandonment of civil rights after Reconstruction kept the government's reach far short of the modern state."

**Why it earns the point:** It periodizes the change (Jackson → Civil War → Gilded Age) so the essay has an organizing structure, takes a position on extent ("enormously... yet bounded"), and names the continuities that limit the claim.

**What a restatement looks like (earns nothing):** "The role of the federal government changed between 1830 and 1910." No position on how far, no periods, no line of reasoning.

## 2. Contextualization scope

The expansion needs its setting: Jacksonian democracy's suspicion of concentrated power (the Bank War as the defining statement); the Civil War as the great expander of federal capacity; industrialization creating national-scale economic problems individual states couldn't handle; the Progressive response to corporate power. Keep the window strictly 1830–1910 — context can touch the founding-era debates over federal power, but the New Deal is far outside and must not appear.

## 3. Evidence areas

### Evidence the role changed
- **From Jacksonian restraint to Civil War activism:** the Homestead Act (1862), the Morrill Land-Grant Act (1862), and the Pacific Railway Act (1862); greenbacks (1862) and the National Banking Acts (1863–64); Reconstruction — the Freedmen's Bureau (1865) and the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments.
- **Gilded Age and Progressive regulation:** the Interstate Commerce Act (1887) and the Sherman Antitrust Act (1890); Progressive-era railroad, food, and drug regulation (the Hepburn Act and the Pure Food and Drug Act, both 1906).

### Evidence of continuity and limits
- **Laissez-faire survived:** the Supreme Court narrowed the Sherman Act (*United States v. E.C. Knight*, 1895) and struck down labor regulation (*Lochner v. New York*, 1905).
- **Retreat on civil rights:** Reconstruction's end (1877); *Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896) — the same government that regulated railroads would not protect Black voting rights.
- **No welfare state:** no federal income security or labor standards of modern scope; the government's growth was economic and military, not social.

## 4. Complexity pathways

1. **The role split rather than simply growing.** The government expanded dramatically in economic management while staying minimal in social welfare and civil rights — a student who argues the "role" changed in different directions at once, rather than in one direction, earns complexity.
2. **Crisis-driven change.** The biggest expansions came in wartime (the Civil War) rather than from steady principle — qualifying *how deliberate* the change was, and suggesting a different causal story than steady reform.
3. **Reform and resistance as simultaneous.** The same era produced antitrust law *and* the Court decisions gutting it — a student who treats expansion and limitation as concurrent, not sequential, shows a genuinely nuanced "how far."
