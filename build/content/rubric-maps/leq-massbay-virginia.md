# Rubric Map — LEQ: Massachusetts Bay vs. Virginia, 1607–1754

**Source file:** `build/reclaim-merged/staged/princeton-25e/leq-test2.json` (id: `leq-test2-spare-a`)
**Test:** test-03
**Prompt:** "Assess how far the Massachusetts Bay Colony differed from the Virginia colony between 1607 and 1754."
**Targeted reasoning:** Comparison

**Rubric reminders:** 6 points — thesis (a defensible claim taking a position on *how far* they differed, more than a restatement); contextualization (broader context: the English colonization wave, joint-stock ventures, Reformation aftershocks, Native displacement); evidence (at least 2 specific examples = 1 pt; used to support the argument = 1 pt); analysis & reasoning (comparison structures the argument — differences *and* similarities weighed against each other = 1 pt; complexity = 1 pt).

## 1. Thesis framing

**Strong example thesis:** "Massachusetts Bay and Virginia differed fundamentally in founding purpose and social order — a Puritan religious refuge built on towns and family farms versus a profit-driven plantation colony built on bound labor — though both shared an English legal and political identity and both expanded at Native peoples' expense."

**Why it earns the point:** It names the basis of comparison (purpose, economy, labor, religion and government), takes a position on extent ("differed fundamentally"), and keeps the comparison two-sided with the similarity clause. The essay can be organized around those named categories.

**What a restatement looks like (earns nothing):** "The Massachusetts Bay Colony differed from the Virginia colony between 1607 and 1754." No position on how far, no categories of comparison, nothing to defend.

## 2. Contextualization scope

The comparison needs its setting: the early-1600s English colonization wave, with joint-stock companies as the standard vehicle; the Protestant Reformation's aftershocks pushing Puritans out of England; England's mercantilist interest in staple exports; and the wider pattern of Native displacement as English settlement expanded. The window opens at Jamestown (1607) and closes before the imperial crisis (1754) — no Revolution material; the comparison lives entirely in the colonial period.

## 3. Evidence areas

### Evidence they differed
- **Founding purpose:** Virginia — a Virginia Company joint-stock venture (1607) organized for profit; Massachusetts Bay — a Puritan religious refuge (company charter 1629, Winthrop's fleet 1630), the "city upon a hill."
- **Economy and settlement:** Virginia — tobacco plantations from the 1610s, dispersed settlement, few towns; Massachusetts Bay — mixed farming, fishing, shipbuilding, and trade in compact towns.
- **Labor:** Virginia moved from indentured servitude to enslaved African labor (first Africans 1619; slave codes hardening by the late 1600s); Massachusetts Bay relied on family and town labor, with slavery present but small-scale.
- **Religion and government:** Virginia — Anglican establishment and the House of Burgesses (1619); Massachusetts Bay — Congregational establishment, town meetings and the General Court, with the early franchise restricted to church members.

### Evidence of similarity (keeps the comparison honest)
- Both carried English law, political traditions, and loyalty to the Crown.
- Both displaced Native peoples: the Anglo-Powhatan Wars (1622, 1644) in Virginia; the Pequot War (1636–37) and King Philip's War (1675–76) in New England.

## 4. Complexity pathways

1. **Convergence over time.** By 1754 both colonies were more "Anglicized" — Virginia had an established gentry and parish system, Massachusetts had wealthy merchants and a royal governor — so differences were sharpest early and softened. A comparison with a time dimension.
2. **Ranking the differences.** A student can argue which difference mattered most — e.g., that labor systems shaped everything else (wealth, hierarchy, settlement pattern) — weighing causes inside the comparison rather than listing them evenly.
3. **Shared Englishness as a limit.** Both were variations on an English colonial model — common law, Protestant Christianity, patriarchal households — which qualifies the "fundamentally differed" claim: the student argues the differences were real but bounded by a common template.
