## 2. Per-transcript structure, devices, and style

### T1 — Speed Review (exam-tool format)
- **Opens** with a study contract, not content: print the review sheet, circle what you
  don't know, check off what you've mastered. The viewer is put to work in 30 seconds.
- **Order:** pre-contact Native regions → Columbus pivot (sponsors + motive) → Columbian
  Exchange (both directions, then effects) → papal/Tordesillas frame → conquistadors and
  Cortés → encomienda → missionaries → casta → Valladolid (both biographies) → exam forecast.
- **Closes** by predicting question types: causes of exploration, Exchange effects,
  region-vs-region and debate-vs-debate comparisons. Then the product pitch.
- **Pacing devices:** the checklist device (circle/check), hard pivots on dates, and a
  recurring "here's why this matters for the test" lens.
- **Memory hooks:** the motive triad (gold/God/glory); the "Great Dying" label; the
  Las Casas–Sepúlveda pair presented as the exam's favorite comparison.
- **Humor:** none — warm but dry delivery ("hey students" energy, no jokes).
- **Emotional beats:** one — the disease collapse. Everything else is flat exposition.

### T2/T3 — Two-Minute sprint (compression format)
- **Opens** cold on the date: period begins 1491, Native cultures, no preamble at all.
- **Order:** 1491 Natives (two sentences) → 1492 pivot → Exchange defined → Europe gains /
  Native losses → conquistadors/Cortés → encomienda → casta → Valladolid → outcome
  (asiento) → product pitch. A straight chronological chain, zero detours.
- **Pacing devices:** date anchors as the skeleton (1491 → 1492); dictionary-style
  definitions dropped inline ("this refers to…"); an authority cue that flags Cortés as
  the exam's preferred example.
- **Memory hooks:** same motive triad; the date pair 1491/1492 as bookends.
- **Humor:** none. **Emotional beats:** none — pure information density.
- **Note:** T3's Period 1 section is the same underlying script as T2 (topic sequence
  identical; caption quality differs). The studio reuses the sprint script inside the
  compilation — evidence the format is their workhorse.

### T4 — Midterm section (thesis format)
- **Opens** with exam framing (first-semester scope, take notes) then states a thesis for
  the whole period: exchanges and conflicts from first contact. The only transcript
  with an interpretive frame up front.
- **Order:** thesis → three named Native groups (Pueblo, Plains, Iroquois) → Exchange
  itemized by direction → 90% collapse → European pop growth → Atlantic slave trade →
  encomienda → the Spanish self-justification (protection + faith) → Valladolid as a
  human-rights conflict → Pueblo Revolt resistance beat → test-tips close.
- **Pacing devices:** itemized lists (crops this way, animals/disease that way); "focus
  on…" directives at the close.
- **Memory hooks:** the "exchanges and conflicts" thesis itself; the 90% figure; the
  Valladolid-as-human-rights reframe.
- **Humor:** none.
- **Emotional beats:** two — the catastrophe (90%), then the only triumph beat in any
  transcript: the 1680 revolt temporarily expelling the Spanish. Oppression → agency.

### Cross-cutting observations
- All four are exam-delivery vehicles: the viewer is a test-taker, never a traveler.
- None use narrative scenes, characters, dialogue, or primary sources.
- None use humor, story, or sensory detail — engagement comes from density and clarity.
- T1 is the most complete; T4 is the most analytical; T2/T3 is the most efficient.

## 3. Strengths to absorb (as principles) and weaknesses to avoid

### Absorb — stated as principles, not lines
- **Open with a contract or a thesis, never throat-clearing.** T1's study sheet, T4's
  "exchanges and conflicts" — the viewer knows the deal in 20 seconds. Our version:
  open every video with its narrative question on screen.
- **Date anchors as skeleton.** 1491 → 1492 → 1607 carries the whole period in three
  beats. Keep the anchor habit; add our own (1200 Cahokia, 1680 revolt).
- **Triads for memory.** Motive-triad, three Native groups, three video chapters —
  the brain keeps threes. Use deliberately, not accidentally.
- **Name the exam's favorite example out loud.** T2/T3 flags Cortés explicitly. We do
  the same for ours (Cahokia, Potosí, Valladolid pair) — it builds trust with students.
- **Argue both sides in their own terms.** T4's best move: the Spanish justification
  (protection + faith) is stated fairly before the debate. That teaches argument, not
  just facts. Required for our Valladolid video.
- **End oppression with agency.** T4's Pueblo Revolt beat is the only emotional lift
  in all four transcripts. Any conquest narrative we write closes on resistance, not
  victimhood.
- **Close with a forecast.** T1 predicts the question types. Our outro card does the
  same work visually ("what the exam asks here").

### Avoid — gaps and failure modes spotted
- **The missing motive.** T2/T3/T4 never explain WHY Europe sailed (no F&I, no
  Tordesillas, no gold/God/glory context in T4). Columbus "arrives" without a cause.
  Our Ch 1.2 exists precisely to fill this.
- **The missing resistance.** T1/T2/T3 end Native history at victimhood. Never do this.
- **Name-drop empires.** T1/T2 name Aztec/Inca/Maya but explain nothing about how any
  of them worked. A name without a mechanism is trivia. Every society we name gets its
  organizing fact (maize → surplus → hierarchy).
- **The oversimplified handoff.** T2/T3's "debates ended encomienda, replaced by
  asiento" compresses a century of economics, resistance, and demography into one
  clause. We show the mechanism (dying Native labor force + profit motive), not the
  slogan.
- **Zero humans.** No transcript has a single person speaking, deciding, or feeling.
  Everyone is a category ("the natives," "the Spanish"). Our scripts put named people
  with motives on screen (de Bry's engraver, Popé, Las Casas the regretful advocate).
- **Zero numbers.** Only one figure (90%) across all four. Our counters (20,000 at
  Cahokia; tons of Potosí silver; miles of trade routes) are a differentiator —
  numbers make scale real.
- **Flat affect.** No humor, no wonder, no dread anywhere. The bar is low: any genuine
  energy — a paradox opener, a well-timed pause, a second voice — beats all four.

## 4. Recommended convergence — our 5 videos

  with T4's named-group specificity (Pueblo, Iroquois, Plains as characters, not
  categories).
- **Go beyond all four:** Cahokia and the Mississippians — absent from every
  transcript — become the Eastern anchor. Eastern Woodlands get real detail (not just
  "longhouses"). Every society named gets its organizing fact, per the anti-trivia rule.

### vid-u1-02 "Three Ways to Live in America" (Ch 1.1b)
- **Best model:** none — no transcript uses a comparative "three ways" frame; T1's
  region-vs-region exam forecast is the closest cousin.
- **Go beyond all four:** this is our signature video. The causal chain none of them
  articulate — maize → surplus → staying put → hierarchy → cities — is the thesis.
  T1 proves students can handle the maize depth; we turn it into the engine of the
  whole chapter.

### vid-u1-03 "Why Europe Sailed West" (Ch 1.2)
- **Best model:** T1 — the only transcript that covers motives, sponsors, and
  Tordesillas at all.
- **Go beyond all four:** T1 compresses this into two clauses. We give it a full
  video: the Reconquista mindset, the Ottoman price squeeze, Prince Henry's school,
  F&I's gamble, the papal line, Portugal's African trade. The motive-triad becomes a
  recurring refrain, not a throwaway. This video answers the question three of four
  transcripts never ask.

### vid-u1-04 "Conquest, Silver, and the Exchange" (Ch 1.3a)
- **Best model:** T4's itemized Exchange (most specific directional lists) + T2/T3's
  tight Cortés anchor + T1's labor systems (encomienda → casta).
- **Go beyond all four:** Potosí silver — the economic engine of the Spanish empire,
  absent everywhere. Two-way Exchange with real items and real stakes (the potato in
  Europe, the horse on the Plains, sugar and slavery in the Caribbean). Show the
  mechanism of the labor handoff (collapsing Native workforce + profit), not the
  one-clause version.

### vid-u1-05 "Who Had the Right?" (Ch 1.3b)
- **Best model:** T4's framing — Spanish self-justification stated fairly, then the
  debate as a human-rights conflict, closed with the Pueblo Revolt agency beat.
- **Go beyond all four:** Las Casas's tragic irony (he once proposed African labor,
  then spent his life regretting it) — the moral complexity none attempt. Sepúlveda
  argued from Aristotle, not cruelty — steelman him, then let Las Casas answer. Close
  the unit on 1680: the only transcript with this beat is T4, and it proves the
  closer works.

## 5. Delivery design for TTS (fun, emotional, human)

The transcripts are monotone by design (exam delivery). Our edge is performance.
Principles for the Fish / Edge TTS scripts, scene by scene:

- **Cold opens land the paradox first.** Never "hey students, today we learn…". Open
  mid-thought: a number that shouldn't exist, a question with no good answer, a scene
  already in motion. (Principle absorbed from T1's contract-open, sharpened.)
- **One emotional arc per video.** u1-01: wonder. u1-02: the click of understanding.
  u1-03: ambition. u1-04: awe curdling into horror. u1-05: moral argument → defiant
  lift. Mark the arc in the script's stage directions so the TTS voice knows where it
  is.
- **Pace is punctuation.** Fast, clipped delivery for lists and triads (Exchange
  items, the three ways); slow down to half speed for landings (the 90%, "complexity
  is fragile", Popé's revolt). Write [slow]/[pause] tags into the script — the
  pipeline already parses them.
- **Two voices where it helps.** The debate video (u1-05) wants two distinct voices
  for Las Casas and Sepúlveda — argument you can HEAR beats argument you hear about.
  Edge TTS gives us the casting; Fish gives us the emotion. Use both deliberately.
- **Direct address sparingly, at pivots.** "Picture this," "here's what the exam
  wants," "now freeze this picture" — T1/T4 prove these phrases orient students;
  ration them to scene turns so they keep their power.
- **Refrains, not catchphrases.** Repeat the structural idea, not a joke: "the
  environment sets the menu," "surplus is the mother of hierarchy." Three repetitions
  across a video is a memory hook; five is a slogan. Stay at three.
- **End every video on a forward lean.** T1's exam forecast and T4's resistance beat
  both point past the credits. Our end cards do this visually; the voiceover's last
  line must hand off to the next video ("next: Europe arrives").
