Marcus: May 1754, the Ohio Valley. A 22-year-old Virginia militia officer named George Washington is marching a small force into the Ohio country, claimed by Virginia's governor and already being fortified by the French.

Maya: Twenty-two. I couldn't parallel park at twenty-two.

Marcus: May 28th, before dawn, in the rain: Washington's men surround a French camp at Jumonville Glen and open fire. The Sieur de Jumonville is killed, and some accounts say he carried a diplomatic message, not attack orders.

Maya: So who actually fired first?

Marcus: Nobody's fully sure. The French called it an assassination, and when Washington surrendered Fort Necessity five weeks later, the terms he signed used the French word for it, l'assassinat. He said he hadn't understood the translation.

Maya: He signed a confession he couldn't read. That one had to sting.

Marcus: It gets rougher. London and Paris are already circling each other, and this skirmish lights the fuse. A year later, Britain sends a real army: General Edward Braddock, redcoats in European formation marching through Pennsylvania woods toward Fort Duquesne.

Maya: This is why my paintball career lasted one afternoon: I led a tactical advance across an open field, and one kid behind a tree took out all four of us cousins. I still hear about it at Thanksgiving.

Marcus: Braddock's men learned it worse. July 1755, near the Monongahela: French and Native fighters fire from the tree line, the column collapses, Braddock is mortally wounded. Washington rides out alive, writing home of "four bullets through my coat, and two horses shot under me."

Maya: Four bullets through his coat and he walks away. Okay, that's going on the episode sheet.

Marcus: And the war goes global: Europe, India, the Caribbean, even Manila in the Philippines.

Maya: The Philippines? This war had a Pacific theater?

Marcus: It had every theater. Some historians call it the first world war.

Maya: Wait, before we leave 1754: the Albany thing. The colonies tried to team up, right?

Marcus: June 1754, Albany: seven colonies send delegates to coordinate defense, and Franklin proposes one united colonial government. The Albany Plan of Union.

Maya: And London vetoed it, because London always vetoes everything.

Marcus: London did say no, but the colonies said no first. Every assembly rejected it: nobody wanted to pay for it, and nobody wanted to give up power. The moment they most needed to act together, they couldn't.

Maya: Box one: America couldn't unite, Britain couldn't win in the woods, and a 22-year-old started all of it. Checking that one.

Marcus: Then 1757: William Pitt the Elder takes charge of the war and flips the strategy. Instead of bleeding in Europe, he pays Prussia — subsidies to Frederick the Great — to keep France tied down there, and pours Britain's money and men into North America.

Maya: Conquer America in Germany. I remember that line.

Marcus: That's the line attributed to Pitt, though the wording is debated. The strategy isn't: hold France in Europe with Prussian bayonets Britain paid for, and win across the Atlantic.

Maya: Hold on: 1757, the woods, the French. This is The Last of the Mohicans, right? The Daniel Day-Lewis movie?

Marcus: I've never seen it.

Maya: That changes this weekend: the siege of Fort William Henry, same war, same year.

Marcus: Then you'll know what 1759 felt like. September 13th, before dawn: Wolfe lands his men below Quebec, scales the cliffs in the dark, lines up on the Plains of Abraham. The French commander Montcalm marches out to meet him instead of waiting behind the walls.

Maya: So they starved the city out. Months of siege, right?

Marcus: The siege ran all summer. But the battle that decided it lasted something like fifteen minutes, and both commanders were dying by the end of it: Wolfe on the field, Montcalm by the next morning. Quebec fell, and Montreal went the year after. New France was finished.

Maya: Fifteen minutes to end an empire. Wild. So France is done in North America. What does Britain actually get?

Marcus: February 1763, the Treaty of Paris: France gives up Canada and nearly everything east of the Mississippi; Spain gives up Florida. Britain's North American empire roughly doubles in a day.

Maya: And the bill?

Marcus: Roughly doubles too: the war piles something like sixty million pounds onto what Britain already owed, and the debt lands around 130 million. London now owns a continent it can't afford.

Maya: So they won the world and went broke doing it. That's the whole episode in one sentence.

Marcus: It's the whole next unit in one sentence. But first, the people the treaty forgot. Spring 1763, the French barely gone, and the Ohio country explodes: an Ottawa leader named Pontiac organizes a rising: first the siege of Detroit, then British forts falling across the Great Lakes and the Ohio Valley.

Maya: Because the British acted like they'd conquered the Native peoples too, not just the French.

Marcus: Exactly. The French had been trade partners: gifts, diplomacy, marriages. The British ended the gift diplomacy and raised prices, treating the Ohio peoples as a defeated enemy. They weren't. Pontiac's Rebellion burns through 1763, and London panics.

Maya: And the panic has a name: the Proclamation Line.

Marcus: October 1763: no colonial settlement west of the Appalachians. London draws a line on the map to stop the fighting and, just as much, to stop paying for frontier wars. The colonists who'd just fought for that land are furious. The land speculators most of all, including Washington.

Maya: Wait. Washington the land speculator?

Marcus: Washington the surveyor turned speculator, with claims all over the Ohio country. The Proclamation froze them. One more crack between London and the colonies.

Marcus: And historians still argue about this one: was it a European war that happened to be fought in America, or an American war that went global? The answer you pick changes whose story it is.

Maya: I'm going to say both, and let the historians fight.

Marcus: Most would accept that. Either way the double legacy stands: victory and debt. Britain got the continent and the bill, and the bill is what breaks the empire.

Maya: Recap's next, then I test myself.
