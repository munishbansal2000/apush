# APUSH Fish Direction Catalog

Every bracket tag below is passed straight through to Fish Audio at render
time. Author them inline in the scripts: one tone tag at the start of a
turn, paralanguage inline where the moment earns it. The pause tags
(`[N-second pause]`) are production notes for real silence — they never go
to the voice.

**Standing rules**
- One tone tag per turn, max. Two is clutter.
- No single tone on more than ~40% of a lesson's turns. Vary it.
- `[professional broadcast tone]` is for cold opens, formal framing, and
  sign-offs — never the body default.
- Self-test questions and model answers stay neutral. No direction tags
  there; the content carries it.
- Paralanguage (`[chuckle]`, `[sigh]`, `[beat]`…) max 2 per lesson, max 1
  per scene — only where the moment genuinely earns it.
- `[speaking slowly]` is for numbers, dates, and terms the listener must
  catch exactly — not for whole turns.

## Tone tags — the catalog

### The workhorses (expect these on most turns)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[conversational]` | The default body delivery. Natural back-and-forth, no special color. Maya and Marcus live here. |
| `[curious, inquisitive tone]` | Maya's genuine questions. "Wait, why does that work?" moments. The engine of the interview. |
| `[confident tone]` | Landing a takeaway. "Here's the rule." Marcus stating the exam truth. |
| `[measured]` | Careful exposition. Building an argument step by step. Marcus explaining a chain of causes. |
| `[thoughtful tone]` | Nuance, trade-offs, verdicts. "It depends." The close of a both-sided beat. |

### Story and feeling
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[warm tone]` | Human stories, empathy, reassurance. The Joads, the breadlines, a student's anxiety. |
| `[serious tone]` | Bad news and consequences. Crackdowns, casualties, the Depression's human cost. |
| `[dramatic]` | High-stakes moments. The crash, Pearl Harbor, the missiles in Cuba. |
| `[ominous]` | Foreshadowing. The storm gathering — "and then the banks started to fail." |
| `[calm]` | Steady reassurance after heavy material. The breath after the storm. |
| `[intrigued]` | Leaning into a mystery or hook. "Here's the strange part." |
| `[surprised]` | Genuine surprise at a reveal. The number nobody expects. |
| `[impressed]` | Genuine admiration. "That's actually brilliant." |

### Energy and momentum
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[speaking with mild excitement]` | Genuine positive reveals and wins. "This actually works." |
| `[excited]` | High-energy reveals. Sparingly — the exclamation point of the catalog. |
| `[energetic]` | Upbeat momentum through a fast-moving sequence. |
| `[quickening]` | Building momentum toward a reveal. The pace picks up. |
| `[building]` | An argument accumulating weight, point by point. |
| `[driving]` | Propulsive narration through a chain of events. Timelines, cascades. |

### Maya being Maya (messy, human, wrong)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[incredulous]` | "Wait, that can't be right." Maya pushing back on something absurd-but-true. |
| `[sheepish]` | Maya caught being wrong. Her wrong-beat moments — owns it, doesn't hide. |
| `[playful]` | Banter and light teasing between Maya and Marcus. |
| `[catching]` | Catching a mistake mid-stream — the perky self-correction. "Wait, no —" |

### Marcus being Marcus (the expert)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[firm]` | Stating the exam truth with no hedge. "The crash didn't cause the Depression." |
| `[stern]` | Correcting firmly. The myth-bust landing. Rare — stern is a spice. |
| `[emphatic]` | The punchline of a correction. The word the student must not forget. |
| `[reasonable]` | "Let's think this through." Walking back from a hot take. |

### Debate heat (Volkov, Henry, and the big fights)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[passionate]` | Oratory. Volkov making Moscow's case, Henry on the floor. Full voice. |
| `[intense]` | Debate heat. Both closers rebutting. The temperature rises. |
| `[heated]` | Debate peaks. Almost never — heated is the fire alarm of the catalog. |
| `[fierce]` | Strong disagreement, controlled. Rare. |
| `[cold]` | Chilling facts delivered flat. "Fifteen thousand sailors." Rare and devastating. |

### Wit and edge (sparing)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[deadpan]` | Dry humor, ironic observations. "The tariff was gasoline on a fire." |
| `[dry]` | Dry wit variant — the aside that lands sideways. |
| `[sarcastic]` | A dry edge. Very sparing — sarcasm curdles fast in teaching. |
| `[darkly amused]` | Ironic historical observations. "The loop had no real money in it." |
| `[cool]` | Understated delivery. The opposite of selling it. |

### Precision
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[speaking slowly]` | Numbers, dates, defined terms the listener must catch exactly. Short bursts, not whole turns. |
| `[professional broadcast tone]` | Cold opens, formal framing, sign-offs. NOT the body default. |
| `[casual]` | Asides and throwaway lines. Jay's natural register. |
| `[low voice]` | Conspiratorial asides. Secrets, backroom deals. Rare. |
| `[triumphant]` | Victories and breakthroughs. Almost never in APUSH — history rarely cheers. |

## Paralanguage (inline, max 2 per lesson)
| Tag | Use when |
|---|---|
| `[chuckle]` | A genuine small laugh at an absurd-but-true fact. |
| `[laugh]` / `[laughs]` | Shared laughter at a real joke. Almost never scripted. |
| `[sigh]` | Weariness after heavy material. The Depression, the body counts. |
| `[beat]` | A held moment before a reveal. Punctuation you can hear. |
| `[inhale]` / `[exhale]` | Breath before a big turn. Very rare. |
| `[whispering]` / `[whispers]` | Secrets and asides. Very rare. |

## Emphasis
`[emphasis]` goes inline before the single phrase the listener must catch —
the number that matters, the defined term, the contrast word. One phrase
per turn, max. The phrase must read naturally with the tag in front of it.

## Pauses (production notes — real silence, never voiced)
The scripts already use these: `[2-second pause]` through
`[20-second pause]`. CER self-test pauses run 15–20s; prediction beats run
8–10s. Keep them exactly as authored — they are the runtime math.

## Speaker tendencies (starting points, not cages)
- **Maya:** curious, incredulous, sheepish, playful, warm, conversational.
  She asks, she guesses wrong, she feels it.
- **Marcus:** confident, measured, thoughtful, firm, serious, conversational.
  He explains, he lands the takeaway, he corrects.
- **Jay:** casual, curious, sheepish. He learns out loud.
- **Volkov / Henry (debate guests):** passionate, intense, dramatic.
  They argue like their side depends on it.

## Density check (before the render)
Count tone tags per lesson. If any single tag covers more than 40% of
directed turns, re-direct. If `[professional broadcast tone]` appears outside
the cold open, framing, or sign-off, move it. If the self-test wears any tone
tag at all, strip it.
