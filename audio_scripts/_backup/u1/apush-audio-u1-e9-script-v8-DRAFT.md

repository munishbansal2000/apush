Maya: [professional broadcast tone] Last time: the Pueblo Revolt — the conquered answering back, and an empire forced to change. Now: no new boxes. Eight episodes, ten questions, eleven minutes. The dare: fit every answer to the unit's three shapes: what changed, what stayed the same, who pushed back. The eight boxes: Native societies, why Europe sailed, the Exchange, Jamestown, the silver mountain, the labor chain, Valladolid, the Pueblo Revolt. Circle the ones you couldn't explain right now.

Jay: [curious, inquisitive tone] Ten questions. Any mercy today?

Maya: [energetic] Not a drop of mercy. Question one: three Native regions, and how the land shaped them.
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Southwest: Pueblo farmers, adobe, irrigation. Plains: bison hunters, tipis, following the herds. Northeast: mixed farming, hunting, fishing, longhouses.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The engine under all three?
Jay: [casual] Maize, bred from a wild grass and carried north. No maize, no Cahokia.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Cahokia: ten to twenty thousand at its peak, London-sized, around a hundred-foot mound. The Iroquois?
Jay: [casual] Five nations, one council, every nation agreeing. Clan mothers chose the leaders.
Maya: [confident tone] The myth to drop: the untouched wilderness. Box-one mistake: nobody "found" maize. Scoring move: a big pre-contact city means the follow-up is about food. Clean start.

Maya: [energetic] Question two: what got Europeans across the Atlantic? Motives, then tools.
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The three Gs, ranked: gold (spices, silk, the Ottoman squeeze), God (the Reconquista; Granada, January 1492), glory (crowns racing Portugal).
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Lead with the economics. The tools?
Jay: [casual] Caravel, lateen sails, astrolabe, compass, portolan charts, and Henry the Navigator's money. He funded the voyages; the famous school was mostly legend.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The money part was true. The pope's line?
Jay: [casual] Tordesillas, 1494: three hundred and seventy leagues west of Cape Verde. Spain east, Portugal west —
Maya: [conversational] Flip those two. Spain west, Portugal east, which is why Brazil speaks Portuguese. France and England never signed.
Jay: [casual] Right. Spain west. Got it.
Maya: [conversational] I drew a line down the middle of a shared bedroom once. It lasted a day. Bonus: why did Spain fund Columbus when Portugal's math said no?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Granada. Fresh off the holy war, desperate to catch Portugal. Four voyages; he died still insisting he'd found Asia.
Maya: [conversational] The box-two mistake: 1492 isn't just Columbus. Both pieces landed.

Maya: [energetic] Question three: the Exchange. Each way, and the deadliest cargo.
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] West: wheat, sugarcane, horses, cattle, pigs. East: maize, potatoes, tomatoes, tobacco. Deadliest: disease; eight or nine out of ten in the hardest-hit towns.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The one disease that might have gone east?
Jay: [casual] Syphilis, maybe. Some say it crossed from the Americas; others say it was already in Europe. Nobody's settled it.
Maya: [conversational] Name both sides or it's a trap. Crosby named the swap in 1972: no treaty, no vote, the biggest cargo crossed without a ticket. Who gained on the Native side?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The Plains. The horse. The Comanche rebuilt around it; the horse cultures everyone pictures are post-Exchange. Old, but not ancient.
Maya: [conversational] The box-three mistake: sending the livestock east. They all went west. Locked.

Maya: [energetic] Question four: Jamestown. Why did it almost die?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Private money, on a royal charter: the Virginia Company, 1606. Gentlemen hunting gold in a swamp. Smith's rule held until his gunpowder bag sent him home. Winter 1609: five hundred down to sixty.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Spring of 1610. Then what?
Jay: [casual] The survivors quit, and sailed into De La Warr, arriving with fresh colonists. He turned them around.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] What saved it for good?
Jay: [casual] Tobacco. Rolfe's Caribbean strain, around 1612. Then the headright: fifty acres to whoever paid the passage.
Maya: [energetic] The planter who paid. And 1619?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The House of Burgesses, and the White Lion landing "20 and odd" Africans at Point Comfort. The vote and the landing, same year.
Maya: [conversational] Then 1622: Opechancanough's attack, 347 dead, a quarter of the colony. Royal colony by 1624. Pair tobacco with a labor system. No misses yet.

Maya: [energetic] Question five: why was Potosi the engine of the Spanish Empire?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The 1545 strike: Huallpa's wind, or the llama. Thirteen thousand feet, a mint at the mountain's foot, Potosi's own pieces of eight. The crown took a fifth.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The quinto, a full fifth. And the town?
Jay: [casual] Something like 160,000 by the early 1600s, London-sized in the clouds. The labor: the mita, one in seven, mercury on the poorer ore from the 1570s. A forced draft, not slavery.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Don't let an answer choice call it slavery. Who speaks from inside?
Jay: [casual] Guaman Poma: twelve hundred pages, a letter to the king around 1615. The king never saw it.
Maya: Your turn. One mountain, endless silver, the crown borrowing against ships not yet docked. Save it or break it?
[8-second pause]
Maya: It breaks it. Four bankruptcies under Philip the Second. Bodin, 1568: American silver, more money chasing the same goods.
Jay: [casual] So "inflation" alone won't score.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Write the mechanism. The silver that skipped Spain?
Jay: [casual] The Manila galleons: silver west, silk east, Urdaneta's way home. The merchants kept most of it.
Maya: [conversational] The box-five mistake: don't sail the silver east. Five down; we're halfway home.

Maya: [energetic] That closes the what-changed half. Question six: what drove each labor shift?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The encomienda: a town's labor and tribute to a colonist. Disease emptied the towns. The New Laws of 1542 tried to kill the inheritance; the encomenderos killed the viceroy at Anaquito in 1546, and the crown caved.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The New Laws didn't end the encomienda; the encomenderos ended the reform. Then?
Jay: [casual] The drafts: repartimiento in New Spain, the mita in Peru. The crown assigned rotating workers, short stints, wages on paper.
Maya: [conversational] Same idea, two different names.
Jay: [casual] So Spain scaled up the Portuguese machine: Atlantic sugar islands ran on enslaved African labor. The Middle Passage: thousands, then millions; thirteen out of a hundred died crossing.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] The shippers budgeted for the dying. And Las Casas?
Jay: [casual] In 1516 he proposed African labor instead. He didn't start the trade, but he lent it respectability.
Maya: [energetic] The ladder the labor built?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The casta system: peninsulares, criollos, mestizos, mulatos, Indigenous peoples, Africans. Part custom, part law.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] And peninsulares were the American-born ones, right?
Jay: [casual] Spain. Peninsulares were born in Spain; criollos here. You did the thing you warned me about.
Maya: [conversational] I absolutely did. Behind the ladder: the hacienda, peons free on paper, bound by debt.
Jay: [casual] Free on paper, chained by the ledger.
Maya: [conversational] The machine just swapped fuel. The ladder holds.

Maya: [energetic] Question seven: Las Casas versus Sepulveda. The actual disagreement?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Fully human with rights, or natural slaves? Las Casas: rational souls, gave back his encomienda in 1514. Sepulveda: Aristotle's four just causes, never crossed the Atlantic.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] All from his library: Aristotle, Oviedo's chronicles. The four causes?
Jay: [casual] Natural slavery. Idolatry offending natural law. Rescuing the innocents. Carrying the faith.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Valladolid, August 1550, again in April 1551. The verdict?
Jay: [casual] No verdict. Both sides claimed victory.
Maya: [firm] The box-seven mistake: don't write that either side won. Timing trap: the New Laws came in 1542, so the debate was the crown's second try. The afterlife?
Jay: [casual] The Short Account, written 1542, printed 1552: the Black Legend in Dutch and English hands, 1578 and 1583. He wrote it to save the Indians; it became Protestant propaganda.
Maya: [conversational] The debate's boxed.

Maya: [energetic] Question eight: the Pueblo Revolt. Causes, and why the winning didn't last.
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Eighty years of missions: banned kivas, burned masks, forced labor. Then drought, raids, 1675: Treviño arrests forty-seven medicine men. Pope plans five years from Taos.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] And Pope, the Pueblo king?
Jay: [casual] Holy man; the pueblos never had kings. Knotted cords: maguey fiber, one knot per day. August 10th, 1680, through Spanish pens: Naranjo's account.
Jay: [casual] Close to four hundred Spaniards dead, twenty-one priests. Santa Fe besieged; Otermin's reports describe the retreat to El Paso, August 21st.
Maya: [curious, inquisitive tone] Twelve years free. Why didn't it hold?
Jay: [casual] Drought, rivalries, Pope turned theocrat — the Spanish called him a tyrant, exactly what you'd expect the losers to write about the winner. Deposed, re-elected in 1688, dead around 1692.
Maya: Your turn. You're the crown, 1692. A province walked out for twelve years. Vargas reaches Santa Fe: fight or talk?
[8-second pause]
Maya: He talks: pardons, respect for Pueblo lands, bloodless by Spanish accounts. Then 1693, back with colonists, and it's force. Box-eight mistake: writing that Vargas fought his way in, in 1692. He talked in '92; the force came in '93.
Jay: [casual] Spain came back scared: land, courts, tolerated ceremonies.
Maya: [conversational] Fear rewrote the rules. That's the revolt, told straight.

Maya: [energetic] Now who pushed back. Question nine: the New Laws and Valladolid both tried to restrain the conquest. Why did so little change?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] The machine needed workers; every fix threatened the supply.
Maya: [conversational] And the crown's actions say the money came first. Las Casas won the argument — and it changed nothing on the ground.
Jay: [casual] So the question outlived the answer.
Maya: [conversational] The pushback's scored.

Maya: [energetic] Last one: why does 1491 to 1607 shape everything after?
[2-second pause]
Jay: [casual] Contact created a new world on conquest's terms. The biology, economy, and labor of everything after were decided here.
Maya: [conversational] The period thesis. Ten down.

Maya: [conversational] Eight boxes, let's land them. One: Native societies. Land decided food; food decided the move.
Maya: [confident tone] Box one, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Two: why Europe sailed. Gold led, God and glory rode along. Tordesillas: two crowns, everybody else ignored it.
Maya: [confident tone] Box two, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Three: the Exchange. Crops and animals both ways, germs one way.
Maya: [confident tone] Box three, checked.
Maya: [professional broadcast tone] Four: Jamestown. Company money, the starving winter, tobacco. 1619: the Burgesses and the White Lion at—
Jay: [professional broadcast tone] Point Comfort. Don't say Jamestown.
Maya: [confident tone] Point Comfort. Jamestown's the trap answer and I almost took it. Box four, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Five: the silver mountain. 1545, the quinto, the mita, Bodin beat Malestroit.
Maya: [confident tone] Box five, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Six: the labor chain. The grant, the drafts, the crossing, the ladder.
Maya: [confident tone] Box six, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Seven: Valladolid. Four causes from the library, the answer from the field. No verdict.
Maya: [confident tone] Box seven, checked.
Maya: [conversational] Eight: the Pueblo Revolt. The cords, 1680, twelve years free. Talked in '92, fought in '93.
Maya: [confident tone] Box eight, checked.

Maya: Three questions, AP-shaped. Say your answer before I give it.
Maya: One, with a source. A planter writes "20 and odd" Africans … "bought for victuals" at Point Comfort, the same year the Burgesses meet. Why pair them?
[20-second pause]
Maya: Self-government and forced labor arrived together — the Burgesses and the White Lion, 1619. The same tobacco economy that needed bound labor also produced planters demanding a say.
Maya: Two. "The Treaty of Tordesillas divided the world between Spain and Portugal." What's the mistake?
[15-second pause]
Maya: It bound two crowns, and no one else — France and England never accepted the line, so the line only restrained the two crowns that signed it.
Maya: Three. Did Native peoples shape this period, or only get shaped by it?
[20-second pause]
Maya: They shaped it — maize built Cahokia; the horse rebuilt Plains life; the Pueblos held a province twelve years. Pushback is the unit's third shape, and it changed how Spain ruled.
Maya: One more, fast. Potosi's draft: mita or repartimiento?
[5-second pause]
Maya: Mita. Peru's. Repartimiento is New Spain's.

Maya: [professional broadcast tone] Ten questions, eight episodes, one unit —
Jay: [professional broadcast tone] no mercy, and all of them landed.

Maya: [intrigued] Check your eight boxes. Next time: the neighbors move in. Spain, France, Holland, England: four empires, four ways to run a colony.
