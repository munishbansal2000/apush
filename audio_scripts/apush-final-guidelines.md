# APUSH Final Guidelines — the single standard (2026-10-07)

This document supersedes and merges four predecessors:
`apush-script-guide.md`, `apush-validator-checklist.md`,
`apush-uber-fix-list.md`, `apush-fish-direction-catalog.md`
(archived at `_archive/guidelines-superseded-2026-10-07/`).
One doc, no duplicates. The executable companion is `apush-script-gates.py`
(13 gates) — referenced here, not merged.

How it was built: the v11 that survived a high schooler's "ai slop" verdict,
the user's own line-level reviews (U3-E8 v2→v4), the 2026-10-06 freeze, the
2026-10-07 fleet rebuild, the U9-L5 false book-error incident, and the
U1-E3 v9/v10 line-by-line review. Every rule was paid for with a specific
mistake.

---

## 1. Formats

- **Maya + Marcus** (host + expert): the default interview format.
- **Study Buddies** (Maya + Jay): Jay is the student/learner — he guesses
  wrong, asks real questions, learns; never a second expert.
- **Story Mode** (Marcus-led, Maya interjects) and **debates** per the
  lesson map. Voice rules below are written for Maya + Marcus; adapt the
  spirit (real person, never a cue machine) to the format's cast.

## 2. Episode shape

Maya (host, student-surrogate) + Marcus (expert). Hard cap 180 WPM.
Floor 1,440 spoken words; no fixed ceiling — length serves the substance
(episodes run 11–12.5 min when the content earns it; a four-topic debate
earned 14.1). The cold-open time promise, the header, and the actual word
count at ≤180 WPM must all agree — **and "runtime" always means experienced
runtime: spoken words at ≤180 WPM *plus* every second of scripted pause
silence.** Pause tags are production reality, not metadata; a 12-minute
promise must hold with the pauses in.

Maya tracks N boxes on her episode sheet; at most one mid-episode check-in
beat (e.g. "one down") in her voice as the episode earns it — the recap is
the check layer. Box picks: named terms a student could plausibly see as an
MCQ stem or SAQ prompt, spread across the episode's topics. Default 3 boxes
(4 when the content needs it); boxes ≤ topics — fold extra topics under a
box, never leave a topic unboxed. A box owns its sub-topics in the recap: if
headright lives under the tobacco box, the recap says so.

Cold open sells the episode in 30 seconds: one-line continuity nod ("Last
time: …") + collision/stakes line + the boxes + "circle the ones you
couldn't explain" + time promise. Hook inside the first ~45 words — box
admin first, then a dare or a striking image, no exam pitch up front.
Callback density: era + key names, not a plot summary. The cold open's
promises must be delivered by the episode.

Topics play as conversation, never lecture. Transition tease: one line,
Maya's. Never say goodbye before the recap. The forward tease belongs to
the closer, not the transition — one "next time" per episode.

Recap (Maya drives; Marcus corrects; fumbles stay in). Ritual opener: "N
boxes, let's land them." Keep it tight. Two welcome fumble kinds: the
memory-check ("the crown took... twenty percent?") and the content-fumble.
Marcus corrects crisply; never lectures the correction.

Self-test: "Three questions, AP-shaped" ritual framing stays; question →
`[15-second pause]` for CER questions (15–20s; they need real thinking
time), 8–10s for prediction beats → Maya's model answer after the pause.
Answers in claim–evidence–reasoning shape — the logic, never spoken labels
("Claim:/Evidence:/Reasoning:" aloud is bad TTS, banned with no exceptions).
At least one stimulus-style question. With 4 boxes, a fast clearly-labeled
bonus ("One more, fast.") may cover the fourth — the three-question ritual
is never broken silently. Self-test questions and model answers stay
neutral: no direction tags there at all.

Closer: the tagline, shared or varied, in new words every episode. Exactly
once.

## 3. Voice

**Maya** — student-surrogate, not a cue machine. No more than ~60% of her
lines end in `?`. Gets things messily wrong: confident, specific, human —
not textbook-perfect misconceptions every time; sometimes she says something
that goes nowhere. Two different tools: the mid-episode wrong beat (a wrong
guess Marcus corrects in flow) and the recap fumble (memory-check or
content-fumble while landing the boxes). Use both, not two of one. Has one
irrelevant human moment per episode max — a concrete personal image that
colors her reaction, not a non sequitur. Knows something Marcus doesn't at
least once per episode, OR asks something he can't fully answer ("Nobody's
fully sure") — best sourced from pop culture / student-world knowledge, not
mysteriously memorized textbook facts. Says the checkoffs herself ("Checking
that one."). Never echoes Marcus's point back at him — new thought or
silence (one shared single-phrase duet per episode max). **Her questions
must be earned**: wrong guesses, objections, pushback — never the exact
question the lesson needs at the exact moment it needs it. No feed lines
that exist only to trigger Marcus's next paragraph.

**Marcus** — expert, teacherly, crisp. Explains; doesn't lecture. No
monologue past ~100 words without Maya breaking in — and the break must
push, not feed ("Give me the extremes," not "how does that break down?").
Reactions vary every time; never "Great question!" twice. Admits
uncertainty ("I don't think that's settled"). Never announces lists.

## 4. The banned list (mechanical — the gates enforce these)

1. Sentence starters `That's` / `Here's` / `Here is`: max 2 per episode.
2. Spoken curriculum labels: "Objective one," "LO1," "causal chain,"
   "historiography," "learning objective." Plain-language equivalents
   ("cause and effect") are fine — the ban is on the jargon term.
3. Empty exam-pitch clichés: "the exam counts/loves…", "shows up on the
   test." Substantive exam devices (§5) are content, not pitch.
4. Balanced triples and parallel closers: one earned chain per episode max,
   and it must carry real logic.
5. Feed lines (see §3).
6. Verbatim repeated taglines within an episode. Cross-episode repetition is
   governed by series grammar (§6).
7. Button-word loops ("Check it." ×3).
8. Aphoristic closers ("That's your whole comparison answer.").
9. Modern analogies (genetic engineering, highways).
10. Standalone micro-turns (under 5 words): max 8 per episode. Mid-turn
    fragments: max 2–3.
11. Uncontracted stiffness (it is, do not) — exempt: emphatic negation,
    and anything inside quotation marks (historical quotes stay verbatim).
12. Twist pivots ("there's a twist"): hard gate, no exceptions.
13. Em dash = TTS beat: max 10 in dialogue (hard gate); the closing
    tagline's dash gets a production note ("do not rush it").
14. "Not X, just Y" antitheses: max 2 per episode (hard gate).
15. Stock transitions ("Which brings us to," "Enter X," "On this much,
    historians agree"): retire after one use across the series; the
    fully-retired list is a hard gate — any occurrence fails.
16. Stock phrases: "Fun fact:", "Think about that one for a second" —
    retired, do not use.

## 5. Exam devices

Substantive devices that teach exam thinking are standard in every episode:
- **Prediction beats (1–2):** "Your turn." + real pause (8–10s). Answerable
  by reasoning, not trivia recall, at a natural decision point. Never let a
  speaker say "Pause." aloud. (Video pipeline may add an on-screen reveal;
  audio scripts don't need it.)
- **Exam tips:** varied templates, never the same opener twice; each must
  address a REAL student error. No template monoculture.
- **Common-mistake traps:** one per box, real errors only — taught as LIVE
  TRAPS (Maya makes the mistake, Marcus catches her), never as
  announcer-voice proclamations ("Common mistake, box two:…"). The device
  lands as experience instead of recitation. (2026-10-07: supersedes the old
  "common-mistake lines" wording.)
- **Self-test:** §2. CER logic in natural connective tissue, never spoken
  labels.

## 6. Series grammar (cross-episode repetition)

- RITUAL (keep consistent): the cold-open box circle, the recap opener ("N
  boxes, let's land them."), the self-test framing. Students should feel the
  shape repeat.
- TISSUE (vary every episode): tagline closer, transition lines,
  topic-level language, Maya's asides. Never let two episodes share a full
  sentence outside the ritual lines.
- CHECK LAYERS: the recap is the check layer, plus the closing "Check your
  boxes." Mid-episode check-ins: one "one down" beat max.
- Continuity: the cold open's "Last time:" line is the only required
  callback. Deeper callbacks welcome but must earn their place — one per
  episode max. The closer teases ONLY the next lesson; never teach the next
  lesson's content.

## 7. Fact standards (fail-closed — AP audience)

- **Quote the line, or it's unverified.** Every fact-check, every changelog
  "fix," every registry entry carries the exact quoted source line. No
  quote = no verification. (2026-10-07: a Layer-3 agent "confirmed" two book
  errors without reading the book; a v10 changelog claimed two "fixes" that
  were false. Verdicts without quotes are how errors ship.)
- Population/date claims: ranges, not pins ("Around 1100," "10,000 to
  20,000").
- **Tense math.** Present-tense date arithmetic gets checked every time
  ("almost five hundred years old" for 1492→2026 is 534 years — wrong).
- Causal claims: necessity vs sufficiency ("No maize, no Cahokia" is fine;
  "The whole thing ran on corn" gets softened). **No flattened causal
  chains** (the Black Death was flea-borne — "Europe's plagues came from
  domesticated animals" overstates; Atlantic-island slavery predates Potosí
  silver — "germs, then silver, then chains" is a false ordering).
- "Most of" beats "all." Debated claims get attributed in-dialogue.
- **Attribution strength.** No "one textbook estimates," no "historians
  say," no "one account." Name the estimate and its source.
- **Name the mediator and the actor.** Every primary-source voice carries
  its mediation chain (the Florentine Codex was compiled by Sahagún with
  Nahua scholars — say so). No passive-voice erasures (Africans as cargo
  without the African states' roles in the trade).
- **The exception trap.** Every lesson teaches its rule-breaker (the turkey
  went east while every other domesticated animal went west — an AP-savvy
  student will find a missing exception).
- Don't invent second-order detail. Voice the hedge on a ladder matched to
  the uncertainty: "about" → "roughly" → "something like" → "the leading
  theory" / "nobody's fully sure." Don't pin what sources don't pin.
- **Framing is factual.** "Technically true but misleading" = wrong. Check
  labels, characterizations, and whether source nuance survived into the
  script. When sources disagree, hedge or present the debate — never
  flatten it. (A source that hedges and a script that flattens = the script
  is wrong.)
- **Book-error claims need the book in hand.** Exact quoted book line +
  independent trusted-source confirmation (Britannica, NPS, Congress.gov,
  State Dept, LOC) before anything is registered or taught. Never register
  from memory of the book. Quoting the book file only proves what the book
  says.
- Every draft gets a dedicated fact pass BEFORE the read-aloud pass,
  checked against sources, not memory.

## 8. The validator's lock bar (the user's own review patterns)

Every Layer-2 and Layer-3 validator runs this list IN ADDITION to the
guide. A script that passes the gates but fails any item is not lock-ready.

- **Quote integrity:** every line presented as a figure's "actual words"
  matches the real wording; reported-not-verified wording is disclosed as
  such. Stimulus quotes exact; splices marked with ellipses. Disclosure
  honestly separates real quotes from dramatized debate dialogue.
- **Exchange logic (debates):** every attack lands on something that
  actually happened in the script. No character quotes a cut line. No reply
  repeats what the same character just said. Reactions follow the line they
  react to.
- **Time-travel:** historical voices stay in their year. Future knowledge
  only from modern voices, with framing that makes that clear. Chronology
  claims verified (who wrote alongside whom; paraphrases match the actual
  argument).
- **Exam devices:** self-test never speaks CER labels. Bonus questions are
  AP-shaped, never trivia. Timing traps explicit, not implicit.
- **Claims:** promise directions correct (winners' promise TO the losers —
  check twice). No overclaims ("every" → "many"); verdicts hedged or
  both-sided. Closers rebut, not just confess.
- **Completeness:** the figure's REAL strongest argument is present.
  Slavery is a live factor where it was one, not a moral aside. No skipped
  causal steps (disease → African labor without the encomienda is a jump —
  name the intermediate).
- **Voice:** no parallel mirror pairs, no "Tell me"/"answer me this" tics,
  no triplets or aphorism chains, no modern-op-ed phrasing in historical
  mouths. Funny true details used where they exist, attributed when the
  humor rests on inference.
- **Structure:** anecdotes at the right point in chronology; flashbacks
  framed as flashbacks. Jokes where energy sags, never mid-theory.
- **Numbers:** real spoken-word count (pause tags stripped); header,
  cold-open promise, and actual agree; pauses counted in the experienced
  promise. Number density reduced — audio can't carry a facts conveyor.

## 9. Fish direction (lessons are directed, not just written)

Full tag catalog below. Direct as you write — tags never change words; a
direction pass that edits dialogue is a rewrite and needs the full loop.

- **Placement:** one tone tag at the start of the turn's text
  (`Marcus: [confident tone] The crash didn't cause…`). Paralanguage inline
  where the moment earns it. Pause tags are production silence, unchanged.
- **Consistency is the rule:** the same dramatic situation gets the same tag
  in every lesson. Cold opens: `[professional broadcast tone]`. Maya's
  genuine questions: `[curious, inquisitive tone]`. Takeaways: `[confident
  tone]`. Myth-busts: `[firm]`/`[stern]`. Maya caught wrong: `[sheepish]`.
  Grim material: `[serious tone]`. Debate guests: `[passionate]`/`[intense]`.
  Numbers to catch: `[speaking slowly]`. A lesson that directs its cold open
  differently from the fleet is wrong — match the fleet, not the whim.
- **Speaker tendencies:** Maya asks, guesses wrong, feels it (curious,
  incredulous, sheepish, playful, warm). Marcus explains and lands
  (confident, measured, thoughtful, firm, serious). Jay learns out loud
  (casual, curious, sheepish). Guests orate (passionate, intense).
- **Density:** no tone on more than ~40% of a lesson's turns.
  `[professional broadcast tone]` only for cold opens, formal framing,
  sign-offs — never the body default. Self-test stays neutral (no tags).
  Paralanguage max 2 per lesson. One `[emphasis]` phrase per turn, max.
- **The gates strip direction tags** before word counts and every check —
  directing never changes runtime math.

### Tag catalog

**Workhorses:** `[conversational]` (default body) · `[curious, inquisitive
tone]` (Maya's genuine questions) · `[confident tone]` (takeaways) ·
`[measured]` (careful exposition) · `[thoughtful tone]` (nuance, verdicts).

**Story/feeling:** `[warm tone]` · `[serious tone]` (bad news) ·
`[dramatic]` (high stakes) · `[ominous]` (foreshadowing) · `[calm]` (breath
after the storm) · `[intrigued]` · `[surprised]` · `[impressed]`.

**Energy:** `[speaking with mild excitement]` · `[excited]` (sparingly) ·
`[energetic]` · `[quickening]` · `[building]` · `[driving]`.

**Maya:** `[incredulous]` · `[sheepish]` (caught wrong) · `[playful]` ·
`[catching]` (mid-stream self-correction).

**Marcus:** `[firm]` (exam truth, no hedge) · `[stern]` (myth-bust; rare) ·
`[emphatic]` · `[reasonable]`.

**Debate heat:** `[passionate]` · `[intense]` · `[heated]` (almost never) ·
`[fierce]` (rare) · `[cold]` (rare, devastating).

**Wit (sparing):** `[deadpan]` · `[dry]` · `[sarcastic]` (very sparing) ·
`[darkly amused]` · `[cool]`.

**Precision:** `[speaking slowly]` (numbers/dates/terms to catch) ·
`[professional broadcast tone]` (opens/framing/sign-offs only) ·
`[casual]` (asides; Jay) · `[low voice]` (rare) · `[triumphant]` (almost
never).

**Paralanguage** (inline, max 2/lesson): `[chuckle]` · `[laugh]`/`[laughs]`
(almost never) · `[sigh]` · `[beat]` · `[inhale]`/`[exhale]` (very rare) ·
`[whispering]`/`[whispers]` (very rare).

**Emphasis:** `[emphasis]` before the single phrase to catch — one per turn
max.

**Consistency audit:** after directing, count tags per lesson and compare
across the fleet. Same beats, same tags.

## 10. Validation (three layers + gates)

1. Write the draft (directed as written).
2. Run `apush-script-gates.py` — fix every FAIL. Gates are the mechanical
   baseline (13 gates, incl. G12 falsehood regression vs the registry and
   G13 spoken-CER-label ban). Agent validations carry the lock; gates don't.
3. **Layer 1 (mechanical):** gates 13/13, confirmed independently before and
   after repairs.
4. **Layer 2 (fresh ear):** a fresh agent that did NOT write or repair the
   script reads every word against §§1–9 of this doc. Line-level findings:
   quote the line, name the rule. The writer never validates their own work.
5. **Layer 3 (fresh fact-checker):** a DIFFERENT fresh agent verifies EVERY
   checkable claim against the source hierarchy — **Tier 1:** the books
   (`books/extracted/5steps2024/`, `premium2027/`, `princeton/`) and
   transcripts (`public_content/`) first; **Tier 2** (only when Tier 1 is
   insufficient): Britannica + NPS. Broad web is NOT a source. **Every
   finding quotes the exact source line** (§7). Unverifiable blocks the
   lock. Judges framing, not just claims.
6. Repairs get a fresh Layer-2 re-read. Then push: draft + changelog +
   registry (same day).

**Registry discipline:** every factual correction enters
`apush-fact-registry.yaml` the same day — correct statement, falsehood
pattern for G12, sources. A correction not in the registry will regress.
After any registry edit, prove it parses.

## 11. Production (TTS)

- Strip before TTS: the `#` header, the read note, every `[N-second pause]`
  tag → real silence. Raw bracket text read aloud is a defect.
- `# Pronunciation:` line in the header with phonetic spellings for every
  risky name/term. Test the riskiest three in the voice before full render.
- Runtime = experienced runtime (§2). If words alone fill the promised
  minutes, trim the speech or lengthen the promise.
- School-safe vocabulary (hard gate): no profanity in dialogue, however
  historical the nickname — find the polite translation and wink at it.
- **Never render an unapproved script.** No audio/video rendering without
  explicit lock and render authorization.

## 12. What the gates can't catch

- Whether Maya feels like a person (read it aloud).
- Whether the facts are right (separate pass, every time — §7).
- Whether it's actually fun (the high-schooler test: one "ai slop" = fail).
