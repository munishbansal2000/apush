  scope leak. NSC-68 survives as exactly one beat (the memo that militarized the

## Factual corrections vs v1 (all checked against Tier 1 before writing)
- Long Telegram taught from the U.S. embassy in MOSCOW, February 22, 1946
  (5 Steps ch25; registry F-U7-003). v1 inherited this correctly; the Princeton
  Review's "duty station in Germany" line is the known book error — not repeated,
  not re-litigated in dialogue.
- X Article: "The Sources of Soviet Conduct," Foreign Affairs, July 1947,
  signed "X" (5 Steps ch25, Princeton ch12). v1 never named it; v2 teaches the
  telegram-then-article chronology as an explicit exam trap (private memo before
  public argument).
- Containment vs rollback: v1 blurred this. v2 teaches the line cleanly and
  anchors it in NSC-68 — Paul Nitze's committee argued America should attempt
  to "roll back" Communism, not simply contain it (5 Steps ch25). Kennan's own
  dismay at the militarization is taught (Princeton ch12: he "lamented the
  militaristic approach").
- Truman Doctrine: March 1947 joint session of Congress, $400M for Greece and
  Turkey (5 Steps ch25, Princeton ch12); the "support free peoples" quotation is
  verbatim per Princeton ch12 and presented as Truman's actual words. The v1
  "blank check" framing is kept but re-anchored in the grammar of the quote.
- Marshall Plan: ~$13B, 1948–1951, 17 nations (Princeton ch12: "almost $13
  billion"); offered to the Soviets, refused by Stalin who compelled the
  satellites to refuse (5 Steps ch25). The generosity-vs-strategy debate is
  taught both-sided ("the rubric wants both"); the phrase "enlightened
  self-interest" is NOT asserted — it does not appear in Tier 1, so the debate
  is carried in plain language.
- NATO: April 1949, twelve founders (US, Canada, ten Western Europeans), Article
  5 collective defense, "the first time that the United States joined a formal
  peacetime alliance" (Princeton ch12). v1 had Maya confuse NATO with the
  Marshall Plan; v2 keeps that confusion pattern out — the money-vs-alliance
  distinction is taught once, cleanly.
- Warsaw Pact 1955 as the mirror, with the trigger Princeton's glossary gives:
  NATO's 1955 decision to invite West Germany. The 1949/1955 gap is an explicit
  exam tip (don't collapse them).
- Ismay line ("keep the Russians out, the Americans in, and the Germans down")
  is presented as the line Ismay is credited with, not a transcript-verified
  quote — corroborated via NATO's own declassified page and the International
  Churchill Society (Tier-2-adjacent). Maya delivers it as her knows-something
  beat. See the Sources footer; disclosed, not taught flat.
