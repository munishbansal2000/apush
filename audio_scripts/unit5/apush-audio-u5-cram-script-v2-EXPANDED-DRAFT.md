# APUSH Unit 5: Cram Session
## Script v2 DRAFT — Maya + Jay (expanded from v1 LOCKED)

**Format:** Cram | **Target:** 1200+ words, ~7 min @ 180 WPM

# Read note: rapid-fire retrieval. Short turns, no lectures. Maya asks, Jay answers, Maya confirms with the exam pointer.

---

Maya: Thirty-second thesis. The slavery question broke every compromise, broke the nation, and was settled by war — but the peace failed to secure what the war had won. If you can explain that sentence, you can write this unit's essay. Ten questions. Rapid fire. Go.

Jay: Hit me.

Maya: 1844. Polk wins on westward expansion. O'Sullivan gave it the name: Manifest Destiny. The idea that America should overspread the continent. Polk wants California, Texas, Oregon.

Jay: Fifty-four forty or fight.

Maya: Oregon. But he compromises at 49. Texas gets annexed. Then the Mexican War. 1846 to 1848. Polk says American blood was shed on American soil. Lincoln says show me the spot. Spot Resolutions.

Jay: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo. 1848. $15 million. Half of Mexico.

Maya: Less than the $25 million Polk offered for California alone before the war. Irony. Then Wilmot Proviso. August 1846. Two sentences. No slavery in the Mexican cession. Passes House, dies in Senate. Never becomes law but breaks the parties.

Jay: Cotton Kingdom. Planter aristocracy, yeoman farmers, poor whites, enslaved people at the base.

Maya: Senate math. Every territory is two future senators. South can't lose the Senate. Then popular sovereignty. Cass's dodge. Let settlers decide. Both sides hate it.

Maya: Question one. Why did expansion make the slavery crisis unavoidable?

Jay: Every territory was two future senators, so every admission was a fight. Texas, the Mexican Cession, then California's gold-rush boom skipping straight to statehood. Both sides saw the West as survival. The South needed room for slavery to grow. The North needed the West free.

Jay: 1848 election. Van Buren, Free Soilers, 10%. Throws New York to Whigs. Taylor wins, dies in 16 months.

Maya: Compromise of 1850. Clay, 73, dying, carried into Senate. Five parts. California free. New Mexico and Utah popular sovereignty. Texas $10 million. DC slave trade banned. Fugitive Slave Act.

Jay: The poison pill. No jury, no testimony, $10 to enslave, $5 to free.

Maya: Five parts plus the poison pill — that's the SAQ answer. The Fugitive Slave Act turned Northern moderates into abolitionists.

Jay: Uncle Tom's Cabin, 1852. Stowe. Best seller. Makes it moral. Webster's speech. "Not as a Massachusetts man but as an American." Costs him everything.

Maya: Kansas-Nebraska. 1854. Douglas wants railroad through Chicago. Repeals Missouri Compromise. Popular sovereignty.

Jay: Bleeding Kansas. Border Ruffians hijack the vote. Two governments. Lawrence sacked. John Brown kills five at Pottawatomie with broadswords. About fifty dead.

Maya: Popular sovereignty in practice. Bleeding Kansas is the SAQ answer for why it failed.

Jay: Sumner caned. Brooks beats him on Senate floor. South sends canes. North outraged.

Maya: Republicans form, 1854. Anti-expansion. Whigs die. Know-Nothings flare and fade.

Jay: Dred Scott. 1857. Three holdings. No Black citizenship. Congress can't ban slavery in territories. Missouri Compromise unconstitutional.

Maya: Three holdings, one sentence each. That's the SAQ answer. Taney tries to settle it. Makes it worse. North sees Slave Power. South demands slave code.

Jay: Lincoln-Douglas debates. 1858. Seven debates. House Divided speech. Freeport Doctrine. Douglas wins Senate, loses South. Lincoln loses, wins presidency.

Maya: Question five. What was the Freeport Doctrine trap?

Jay: Lincoln asked: can a territory exclude slavery before statehood? Douglas said yes. Slavery needs friendly local laws, so unfriendly laws can freeze it out. It won him the Senate seat. But the South heard him gutting Dred Scott, and they never forgave him. He lost the South, the Democrats split at Charleston, and Lincoln took 1860.

Maya: Election of 1860. Four candidates. Democrats split at Charleston. Lincoln 40% popular, 180 electoral. Wins without South.

Jay: Secession. South Carolina December 20, 1860. Six more follow. Cornerstone Speech. It's about slavery.

Maya: Question six. Why secede over Lincoln's election?

Jay: He won with 40% of the popular vote and zero Southern electoral votes. Proof to the South that the political system would never protect slavery again. South Carolina left December 20, 1860, six states followed, and the Cornerstone Speech said it plain: the Confederacy's foundation was slavery.

Maya: Crittenden fails. Buchanan does nothing. Fort Sumter, April 1861. War.

Jay: Why North doesn't win quickly. Better Southern generals. Defensive war. Lincoln can't find a commander.

Maya: Anaconda Plan. Blockade plus Mississippi. Antietam, September 1862. Bloodiest day. 23,000. Opens door for Emancipation.

Jay: Gettysburg and Vicksburg. July 1863. Lee retreats. Mississippi taken. Confederacy split.

Maya: Gettysburg and Vicksburg, July 1863. Know these cold.

Jay: Grant's attrition. Sherman's total war. Atlanta burns. March to sea. Draft riots. Appomattox, April 1865. Roughly 700,000 dead.

Maya: Emancipation Proclamation. January 1, 1863. Paradox. Frees where he has no power. Doesn't free where he does.

Jay: But kills European intervention. Enables 200,000 Black soldiers. Greeley letter. Contrabands. Butler's legal judo.

Maya: Question eight. What did Emancipation actually do — and not do?

Jay: It freed enslaved people in the rebelling states, where Lincoln had no power, and left slavery untouched in the border states, where he did. What it really did: killed European intervention, turned the war into a war against slavery, and opened the door for Black soldiers. About 200,000 served.

Maya: Gettysburg Address. 272 words. Second Inaugural. 701 words. Malice toward none.

Jay: Homestead Act. Morrill Act. Pacific Railway. Greenbacks.

Maya: Reconstruction. Lincoln's 10% Plan. Wade-Davis: a majority, ironclad oath. Johnson takes over. Black Codes. Congress takes over in 1866.

Jay: 13th abolishes. 14th birthright citizenship. 15th Black male vote. Military Reconstruction. Five districts. Johnson impeached, acquitted by one.

Maya: Question nine. Compare the three Reconstruction plans.

Jay: Lincoln's 10% Plan: fast and lenient. Ten percent of 1860 voters swear loyalty, accept emancipation, and the state comes back. Wade-Davis: Congress's answer. A majority of white males, an ironclad oath, Congress in charge. Lincoln pocket-vetoed it.

Maya: And Johnson?

Jay: Johnson's restoration: pardons for most Confederates, new state governments fast, no protection for freedpeople. Then the Black Codes proved it wasn't working, so Congress took over. Military Reconstruction, five districts, the 14th and 15th Amendments. The fight was always who controlled the South's return, and how much the freedpeople got protected.

Maya: Success or failure? Amendments survive. 2,000 Black officeholders. Freedmen's Bureau schools, Shaw University in 1865. But sharecropping, Colfax in April 1873, the Court gutting the 14th, the Compromise of 1877.

Jay: Dunning vs Foner. Mistake vs betrayed.

Maya: Question ten. How did the Compromise of 1877 end Reconstruction — and what replaced it?

Jay: The 1876 election. Hayes versus Tilden, disputed returns in three Southern states. A commission handed them to Hayes, and the informal deal was: Hayes gets the presidency, federal troops leave the last occupied states, Reconstruction ends. What replaced it: Redeemer governments, sharecropping, the disfranchisement machinery, Jim Crow. The war won emancipation. The peace gave most of it back.

Maya: Jim Crow. Poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses. Plessy 1896. Separate but equal. Harlan dissents.

Jay: Ida B. Wells. Exposes lynching. Driven North. Washington vs Wells. Economic vs confrontation.

Maya: DBQ prediction. "Evaluate the extent to which Reconstruction was a success or failure."

Jay: Thesis sketch. Politically, a success: the amendments survived, about 2,000 Black officeholders, public schools. Socially and economically, a failure: sharecropping, the Court gutted the 14th, Jim Crow rebuilt the hierarchy. A revolution half-finished.

Maya: Backup DBQ: was slavery the central cause of the Civil War?

Jay: Thesis: yes. Every crisis from the Mexican Cession to secession was a fight over slavery's expansion, and the South said so itself in the Cornerstone Speech.

Jay: That's Unit 5.

Maya: Expansion, war, betrayal. Know the turning points, know the amendments, know why compromises failed.

Jay: And know the numbers. 1820, 1850, 1854, 1857, 1860, 1863, 1865, 1877, 1896. The dates are the skeleton. Hang the story on them.

Maya: Dates are the skeleton. Story's the muscle. Walk in with both locked. Done.
