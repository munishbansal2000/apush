  cracks). The cultural-nationalism beat (Irving + Hudson River School) was
  elevated to Box 2 as a slim box. Episode arc: build (System) → celebrate
  (culture) → defend (Doctrine) → crack (Panic + Missouri). This deviates
  from the scope brief's listing order (which placed culture last); culture
  after the Missouri bridge would have stepped on the fault-line closer.
- **Box order:** 1. American System (+ Erie Canal symbol), 2. homegrown
  American culture, 3. Monroe Doctrine, 4. the cracks.
- **Missouri is a bridge only.** Per brief: 1819 application, 11–11 balance,
  acrimonious debate — no Compromise content (that's U4-E5's territory).
- **U4-E3 War of 1812:** one continuity sentence in the cold open
  ("Last time: a war nobody won...") plus a motivation reference for the
  American System. No war events re-taught. Marshall Court (U4-E2),
  Louisiana Purchase (U4-E1), Unit 3: not touched.
- **One mid-episode checkoff only** ("Box one... Checking it."); the recap
  is the check layer, plus closing "Check your boxes."
- **Two prediction beats** (8s each): (1) reason out Clay's three parts
  before Marcus names them; (2) why the Doctrine held despite America's
  small forces. Maya gives the model answer after each pause.
- **Self-test:** 3 CER questions (16s each) + "One more, fast." bonus (5s)
  covering Box 2 (culture). Q1 is stimulus-style (the 1817 newspaper
  waterway editorial). No spoken Claim/Evidence/Reasoning labels — logic
  carried by connective tissue.
- **Tagline** (new words, shared duet, once): Marcus "Good feelings on
  the surface —" / Maya "and a fault line running underneath." Forward
  tease (closer only): Missouri per brief's prescribed wording.
