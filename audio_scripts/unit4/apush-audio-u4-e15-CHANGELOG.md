# U4-E15 Changelog — v1 → v2 (2026-10-07)

## Format decision (kept from v1, with tighter role discipline)

Kept the Maya-moderated debate: Marcus argues Polk's case as an advocate
from 1848 (his knowledge stops at the war's end), Rafael is a fictional
composite voicing the Mexican civilian perspective (explicitly framed as a
composite in the read note and in Maya's box-one intro — he is never a
specific historical person), Maya moderates as the modern host.

Why it serves this material:
- The episode's central exam move is contested framing ("American soil").
  A debate puts both maps on the table instead of narrating one.
- Marcus's 1848 cutoff creates honest dramatic irony: he can defend the war
  as Polk saw it but cannot see the slavery explosion coming; Maya carries
  every modern judgment (disease, Grant's memoirs, the verdict).
- Rafael gives Mexico a real argument (the disputed strip, the Niños
  Héroes, half the territory, the treaty's broken promises) rather than
  serving as illustration for the American story. No triumphalism: this is
  a war of conquest, and the script says so.
