## Fact-pass notes (Tier 1 first; every checkable claim below is in prem27 ch8 Topic 6.7 and/or 5steps ch18)
- Conditions: de-skilling/loss of control of work process as root grievance (prem27, both phrasings used); unsafe + unsanitary conditions (prem27); wage cuts after Panics of 1873 and 1893 (prem27); unskilled man $8/week vs skilled woman $5/week (5steps); children 10–15 = 18% of industrial workforce by 1900 (prem27, taught as "nearly one in five"); women 17% (prem27, not taught by number).
- Knights of Labor: founded 1869; welcomed all regardless of race/gender/skill (both); Powderly led late 1880s (prem27); ~750–800k mid-1880s (both; taught as "three-quarters of a million"); broad reform agenda incl. safety rules and ending child labor (prem27); reluctant to strike (5steps "peaceableness"); blamed for radicalism beyond their control after Haymarket (5steps); top-down/autocratic structure blocked new leadership (prem27).
- AFL: 1886; skilled workers only, "aristocracy of labor," craft union vs Knights' industrial union (prem27); bread and butter, no political activity (prem27); Gompers the cigar maker, long-time leader (both); prepared to use strikes (5steps). Taught "exclusive on purpose" — deliberately softer than prem27's "did not allow African Americans or women to join," which the book states flat; flag for L3 whether the flatter book claim should be taught.
- Haymarket 1886: McCormick Reaper Works strike, scabs, May 3 police+Pinkertons opened fire (prem27); May 4 rally at Haymarket Square for the eight-hour day (prem27); bomb into police ranks; police returned fire; eight tried/convicted on scanty evidence, four hanged (prem27; 5steps agrees on eight convicted). **Disclosed, not pinned:** police death toll (5steps says seven officers, prem27 says "several") — taught as "several officers."
- Homestead 1892: Carnegie abroad summer 1892; Frick in control; Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers (craft union under AFL umbrella); contract expired/not renewed; fence, lockout, scabs, Pinkertons; battle "Pinks" vs workers; workers' temporary victory, took the plant; governor called ~8,000 National Guard; Frick reopened without union workers; "devastating blow" (prem27). **Disclosed:** the PA governor is unnamed (not in Tier 1); battle deaths unnumbered (not in Tier 1).
- Pullman 1894: Panic of 1893; wage cuts 1893–94, rents not cut; company town built 1880, company owned all housing, rent deducted from wages (prem27); three organizers fired May 1894; ~3,300 struck; Debs/ARU boycott of Pullman cars; railroads paralyzed; TWO injunctions; Cleveland's federal troops; ~25 strikers killed (taught as "about two dozen"); In re Debs (1895) upheld the injunction; Sherman Act used by courts against union activities in the 1890s (prem27); Debs turned socialist, ran for president multiple times (5steps "several times," taught as "more than once").
- Why labor lost: management's weaponry monopoly + government/courts support + strikebreakers (prem27's exact triad); Haymarket-driven public fear of radicalism (both); 1877 precedent — Hayes sent federal troops (both). Wagner Act 1935 forward line (prem27).
