Maya: Last time: the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, Native peoples answering the conquest question themselves. This time: no new boxes. Eight episodes down. One cram session, three questions the whole unit keeps asking: what changed, what stayed the same, and who pushed back. Circle the one you couldn't answer cold right now. Eight minutes, and you'll land all three.

Marcus: Start with what moved. 1492: Columbus reaches the Bahamas, wrong about the size, not the shape. Two years later, Spain and Portugal draw a line across the Atlantic: Tordesillas, 370 leagues west of Cape Verde. France and England ignored it completely.

Marcus: Crosby gave it a name in 1972: the Columbian Exchange. Potatoes, maize, and tomatoes sail east. Wheat, sugar, and horses sail west. The horse remakes the Plains, the same Plains that used to hunt bison on foot.

Maya: I still can't believe Idaho potatoes are an immigrant story. I killed a cactus once, by the way. In Tucson. A cactus, Marcus. So the part where they moved plants across an ocean and expected dinner — I'm the cautionary tale.

Marcus: The plants were the friendly cargo. Smallpox, measles, influenza mostly sailed west — syphilis might be the one exception, and nobody's fully sure. The toll where it hit hardest: eight or nine in ten. That emptied the villages the conquest marched through.

Maya: Hold on. Disease goes under "what changed"? Nobody ordered that. Change sounds like somebody did something.

Marcus: Nobody ordered it. Which is why it sits first: the change nobody chose, next to the ones people did choose.

Marcus: And the change people chose hardest was silver. 1545: Potosí. A Bolivian mountain, thirteen thousand feet up. It and Zacatecas in Mexico supplied most of the world's silver for the rest of the 1500s. The crown's cut: twenty percent off the top, the royal fifth. The rest flooded the world, prices climbed across Europe for a century, and mercantilism named the obsession: wealth was the pile of silver you sat on.

Maya: "Pieces of eight!" That's the parrot line. Every pirate movie. I finally know what the bird was talking about.

Marcus: I have never seen a pirate movie.

Maya: We're fixing that. But the silver didn't all go to Spain, right? The galleons.

Marcus: Right. From the 1560s, the Manila galleons ran Acapulco to Manila for two and a half centuries, four to six months each way, silver west, silk and porcelain east, China's hunger for silver pulling it all. One world economy, and Madrid was just one customer.

Marcus: Oh, and England spent the unit failing at colonies and stealing ships instead: Drake's privateers, the Armada's wreck in 1588, Roanoke's vanishing act. Then Jamestown, 1607, nearly starved, saved by a weed: Rolfe's tobacco, not gold.

Maya: So much for the king's pamphlet.

Maya: Question one, landed.

Marcus: Question two: what stayed the same. Same empire, same hunger, new paperwork. The encomienda hands a colonist a town's labor. Las Casas pushes the crown to pass the New Laws in 1542. The colonists nearly went to war over it. Gonzalo Pizarro's rebels kill the viceroy sent to enforce them.

Maya: Blasco Núñez Vela. I just said a viceroy's name from memory. Somebody check on me.

Marcus: Consider yourself checked. The crown backed down and scrapped the inheritance ban, and the encomienda kept going. But the crown still needed workers, and disease kept emptying the towns. So it swapped the system instead of the goal.

Maya: And went straight to the hacienda, right? Big estates, debt, done.

Marcus: A century too fast. First the draft, the repartimiento: towns owing a rotating share of workers for a few weeks at a time, assigned by crown officials. When the towns kept emptying, enslaved African labor, the Middle Passage, about one in seven never making it across. The hacienda settles in by the 1600s: big estates, workers technically free, debt doing the chaining.

Maya: Four names, one appetite.

Marcus: And the part that never changes: the crown keeps the machine and swaps the fuel. Was the draft kinder than the grant? Legally, the workers were free men. On the ground? I don't think that's settled.

Maya: Same hunger in Virginia, English accent. Headright land, indentured servants, an elected assembly in 1619. And the same year, the White Lion landed about twenty Angolans at Point Comfort.

Marcus: Question three: who pushed back. First, the pen. Las Casas: held an encomienda, gave it up, spent decades lobbying the crown. The Short Account, finished 1542 — the cruelty on the record, town by town. It helped win the New Laws, barely enforced. And Guamán Poma, an Andean nobleman who drew the same abuses for a king who never read them.

Maya: Decades defending Native peoples, and his pages end up as English propaganda. Plus, around 1516, he proposed African slavery.

Marcus: His 1516 proposal gave the trade a moral cover story — one he spent the rest of his life trying to retract.

Marcus: Then the question went on trial: Valladolid, 1550. Las Casas: Native peoples are rational souls. Sepúlveda: Aristotle's natural slaves, just war. 1550 into 1551, and the judges never ruled. No verdict.

Maya: Sepúlveda, the one who never crossed the Atlantic.

Marcus: Never set foot in the Americas, and built the whole case on Aristotle anyway.

Maya: No verdict. Mock trial flashbacks.

Marcus: In the colonies, the mines kept running. What lived on was the question itself, asked by an empire about its own conquest.

Maya: And then 1680, the answer with teeth. Eighty years of missions, banned kivas, forced labor, then drought the Spanish couldn't fix. Popé's knotted cords: one knot per day, runners across hundreds of miles, everybody rises when the last knot comes loose. August 10th. The Spanish get driven out for twelve years.

Marcus: Close to four hundred Spaniards dead, Santa Fe besieged, Otermín journaling the retreat to El Paso.

Maya: And when Vargas comes back: 1692's handshake, 1693's fight. The empire's chastened. Land grants, a defender for Native cases, ceremonies tolerated underground.

Marcus: Spanish rule returned. The old rules didn't.

Maya: Three questions, let's land them. One: what changed.

Marcus: The crossing: crops both ways and disease one way, with Potosí's silver wiring the planet into one economy.

Maya: Two: what stayed the same. The labor chain: encomienda, then the repartimiento, the mercury-mines one —

Marcus: That was the mita, Peru's draft; New Spain's was the repartimiento.

Maya: Right. Mita in Peru, repartimiento in New Spain. Then African slavery, then the hacienda. Four systems, one hunger.

Maya: Three: who pushed back. The pen and the revolt. Las Casas: the Short Account, the New Laws, the Valladolid question. Guamán Poma's drawings. And Popé's cords, 1680, twelve years of Pueblo New Mexico.

Marcus: And the empire that came back wasn't the one that left.

Maya: All three landed.

Maya: Two questions, AP-shaped. Say your answer before I give it. One: a 1570s mine ledger from Potosí. One column lists the village's share of men for the draft; the margin, three years running: fewer men than last year. Which of the three questions is this source about, and what does the margin tell you?

[5-second pause]

Maya: What stayed the same: the draft grinding on. The margin tells you what was changing underneath: disease emptying the towns, which is why Spain kept inventing the next system.

Maya: Two: name the friar's pen and the revolt, and what the two had in common.

[5-second pause]

Maya: The pen: Las Casas. The revolt: Popé and the knotted cords, 1680. Neither stopped the machine. The friar put the question on the record; the pueblos forced the empire to change how it ruled.

Maya: Prediction time. Two DBQs I'd bet on. One: evaluate the extent to which the Columbian Exchange transformed the Americas. Two: evaluate the relative importance of disease, alliances, and technology in the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire.

Marcus: If the first one lands, your paragraphs are sitting right here: the biology changed, the economy changed — and the pushback never stopped.

Maya: And the counter the graders want: the labor machine ran straight through all of it. New fuel, same engine.
