Maya: Last time: Spain's silver empire, and England's long challenge to it. Drake and Roanoke and the Armada. This time: England stops raiding and starts planting. Jamestown, 1607, the colony that almost didn't make it. Three boxes on your sheet: Jamestown, tobacco, and the House of Burgesses. Circle the ones you couldn't explain right now. Eight minutes, and you'll check all three off.

Marcus: Start with the money behind it. No king paid for Jamestown. A group of London merchants formed a joint-stock company, the Virginia Company. They pooled their cash and split the profits. If the colony failed, the investors lost. If it struck it rich, they got richer.

Maya: So it's a startup. With boats.

Marcus: A startup with boats, yes. In the spring of 1607, about 104 men and boys land on a swampy peninsula up the James River. The site was a choice: easy to defend against Spanish ships, terrible for everything else. Brackish water, mosquitoes, and garbage dumped straight into the river.

Maya: I got sick off lake water on one camping trip, so the brackish-water thing hits different for me.

Marcus: Then you understand dysentery better than they did. Half the colonists were dead within a year. And here's the part that stings: most of them were gentlemen. Sons of gentry, jewelers, stonecutters. Almost nobody who knew how to farm.

Maya: So Jamestown wasn't, like, a town. It was a hundred guys who'd never held a plow.

Marcus: A hundred guys hunting for gold that wasn't there. Which is where John Smith comes in. He gets elected president of the council in 1608 and lays down the rule: he that will not work, shall not eat.

Maya: Cold.

Marcus: Effective. Plant, build, or starve. Then in the fall of 1609, Smith's gunpowder bag explodes, he's badly burned, and he sails home to England. That winter is the starving time. About 500 settlers go in. Around 60 come out.

Maya: Sixty? Out of five hundred?

Marcus: They ate dogs, horses, shoe leather. The archaeology says some ate each other. In the spring of 1610 the survivors actually gave up, boarded ships for England, and got intercepted on the way out by a new governor, De La Warr, with supplies and about 150 fresh colonists.

Maya: Imagine quitting and your boss shows up in the parking lot.

Marcus: With 150 replacements. That's Jamestown's first decade: almost everyone dies, and the company keeps refilling the fort.

Maya: Okay, so how does this place not just die forever?

Marcus: One plant. John Rolfe, who'd landed that spring with the Sea Venture survivors, starts growing a sweeter Caribbean strain of tobacco around 1612. The harsh native leaf nobody wanted. Rolfe's leaf, London couldn't get enough of.

Maya: Wait, the same John Rolfe? Pocahontas's husband?

Marcus: Same guy. Tobacco farmer first, famous husband second. By the mid-1610s, Virginia tobacco is selling in England, and suddenly the colony has a reason to exist. Not gold. A weed.

Maya: Didn't the king hate smoking, though?

Marcus: James wrote a whole pamphlet against it in 1604, calling tobacco a vile custom. The pamphlet lost. The profits won.

Maya: Okay, I have to ask. The Disney version?

Marcus: Forget the singing tree. The real Pocahontas was captured by the English in 1613, married Rolfe in 1614, and that bought a few years of peace. She died in England in 1617, about twenty years old. And the famous rescue, where she saves Smith's life? Smith told that story years later. Whether it happened that way, nobody's fully sure.

Maya: So the whole economy runs on cigarettes.

Marcus: On pipe tobacco, roughly. And tobacco is hungry, for land and for workers. The company answers with the headright system in 1618. Fifty acres to anyone who pays a settler's passage. Bring ten workers, claim five hundred acres.

Maya: So the rich get richer by importing people.

Marcus: That's the engine. And the people came as indentured servants, signing away four to seven years of labor for the boat ride over. Most of them were young, poor, and English.

Maya: Four to seven years. That's a long boat ride to pay off.

Marcus: Later, that labor answer changes. But in the 1610s and 20s, it's servants.

Maya: And who's running this place? The company back in London?

Marcus: At first, yes. Then in the summer of 1619, the company lets the colony elect an assembly: the House of Burgesses. Property-holding men vote, they meet, they make local laws. First of its kind in English America.

Maya: Wait, the company just... gave up power?

Marcus: It traded a little power for a lot of settlers. An elected assembly made Virginia look stable. Stable colonies attract workers, workers grow tobacco, tobacco pays the investors.

Maya: So for the test, 1619 is the democracy year.

Marcus: Half of it. The same year, a Dutch ship, the White Lion, pulls into Point Comfort and trades about twenty Angolans, seized from a Portuguese slave ship, for food. On paper they were sold as servants. In reality they were forced, and they fit every definition of enslaved.

Maya: So that's the start of slavery in Virginia?

Marcus: The start of Africans in Virginia. What students mix up: for decades after this, most field workers were still indentured servants, not enslaved Africans. The full racial slave system comes later.

Maya: Same year. Voting rights and slave ships.

Marcus: The contradiction starts early. And the labor hunger keeps pushing tobacco fields onto Powhatan land.

Maya: So the attack was about land?

Marcus: Mostly land. Tobacco eats territory, and the English kept planting past every boundary line they'd agreed to. In March 1622, Opechancanough, Powhatan's successor, coordinates a surprise attack. About a quarter of the colony is killed in a single day.

Maya: A quarter. So the Powhatan just... snapped?

Marcus: They'd watched the English plant past every boundary for fifteen years. 'Snapped' makes it sound sudden. It wasn't.

Marcus: The English answer with years of war, and the company's charter gets revoked. Virginia becomes a royal colony. But Jamestown survives, because by then tobacco pays for everything.

Maya: Three boxes, let's land them. One: Jamestown. 1607, company-funded, swamp, starving time?

Marcus: 1607, Virginia Company, about 104 settlers. Smith's rule kept them working until his accident in 1609. The starving time left around 60 of 500. De La Warr refilled it in 1610.

Maya: Two: tobacco. Rolfe's sweet strain, and then everybody wants land?

Marcus: Around 1612, Caribbean strain, cash crop. The headright system hands out fifty acres a head, and indentured servants work the fields.

Maya: Three: the House of Burgesses. 1619, first elected assembly, and the same year the White Lion?

Marcus: Summer 1619, property-holding men vote. Same year, about twenty Angolans sold at Point Comfort. Then 1622, the Powhatan attack kills about a quarter of the colony.
