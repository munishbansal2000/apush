# Exam Card — The Valladolid Debate (1550–1551)

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## Positions

- **SEPÚLVEDA** — Natural slavery (Aristotle): Indigenous peoples are born to
  be ruled. Just war against idolatry and human sacrifice. Conquest is mercy:
  faith and law.
- **LAS CASAS** — Rational people, capable of the faith and self-government.
  What he witnessed was Spanish cruelty, not native barbarism. Conversion only
  by peaceful persuasion.

## At stake

**Just war** — can a war of conquest be morally lawful? The first time a
European empire put its own empire on trial. Behind the debate stood the
**encomienda** system: the forced Indigenous labor regime whose legitimacy
both men were really arguing about (addressed by the New Laws of 1542).

## Outcome

No verdict. Both claimed victory; the conquest went on. But the question
survived: conquest itself could be judged by universal moral law — a root of
modern human rights thought (Vitoria, the School of Salamanca).

## Dramatization note

Speeches dramatized from their written arguments (Sepúlveda, *Democrates
Alter*; Las Casas, *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies*).
