# Retrieval Check — Taxation Without Representation (1763–1774)

## 30-second recall

Without looking at your notes: explain the difference between virtual and
actual representation in one sentence each. Then answer: why did the
colonists destroy tea that the Tea Act had made *cheaper*?

## Stimulus-based MCQ

> "The colonists are not represented in Parliament; and no tax can be
> levied on them without their consent, given by their own
> representatives."

The argument in the excerpt most directly reflects which of the following?

(A) The doctrine of virtual representation defended by Parliament
(B) The colonists' claim that only their own assemblies could tax them
(C) The economic argument that British taxes were set too high
(D) The loyalist position that Parliament's authority was absolute

**Answer: (B).** The excerpt makes consent the condition of taxation and
locates that consent in the colonists' *own* representatives — the actual-
representation position. (A) is the opposing doctrine; (C) misreads the
dispute as being about the amount rather than the authority; (D) inverts
the claim.
