# Exam Card — Potosí: The Silver Mountain

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The chain (positions)

- **I. THE DRAFT** — Mita: Toledo repurposes the Inca mit'a (1573). One man
  in seven to the mines for a year; the law promises a wage and a return.
- **II. THE POISON** — Mercury amalgamation refines low-grade ore and keeps
  Potosí producing for two centuries; it poisons everyone who touches it.
- **III. THE INFLATION** — Silver floods Europe; prices rise 3–4× by 1600:
  the price revolution. Spain spends it on wars and goes bankrupt four
  times anyway.
- **IV. THE WORLD** — Silver moves in two streams: Potosí's sails east to
  Europe; Mexican silver sails west from Acapulco aboard the Manila
  galleons (1565–1815) to China for silk and porcelain; up to a third of
  American silver ends up in Asia.

## At stake

**AP Topics 1.4, 1.3** — how one mountain's silver created the first global
economy, and why the empire that mined it went bankrupt anyway.

## Outcome

By 1600 Potosí's silver has inflated Europe, bankrupted Spain four times,
and wired the Andes to China through the Manila galleons.

## Dramatization note

The decree voice paraphrases Toledo's mita terms. The mitayo voice is
dramatized from Guamán Poma de Ayala's account of mine labor (Nueva
corónica, c. 1615), not a verbatim quote. The Huallpa discovery story is
told as legend.
