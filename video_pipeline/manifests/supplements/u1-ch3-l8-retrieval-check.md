## Stimulus-based MCQ

> *Adapted from Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda's Democrates Alter (c. 1547):* "The
> Spanish have a perfect right to rule these peoples of the New World, who in
> prudence, skill, and every virtue fall so far below the Spanish that they
> might be called natural slaves, born to serve rather than to rule."

The argument in the excerpt most directly reflects which of the following?

(A) The Black Legend critique of Spanish colonial cruelty
(B) The application of Aristotelian natural slavery to Indigenous peoples
(C) The legal protections for Indigenous labor under the Laws of Burgos
(D) Las Casas's defense of Indigenous rationality and capacity for the faith

**Answer: (B).** The excerpt argues from nature and hierarchy — some peoples
are "born to serve" — which is Aristotle's natural-slavery doctrine applied to
the Americas, the core of Sepúlveda's case at Valladolid.
