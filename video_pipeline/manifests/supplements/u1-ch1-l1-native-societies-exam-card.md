# Exam Card — Native Societies (North America, 1491)

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## Key points

- **CAHOKIA** — Mississippi valley maize surplus supports a large urban
  center: monumental earthworks, organized labor, long-distance trade.
- **PUEBLO SOUTHWEST** — Aridity answered with engineering: irrigation
  canals, multistory adobe villages around central plazas, clan-organized
  labor. Dry country did not mean scattered country.
- **IROQUOIS CONFEDERACY** — Five nations under the Great Law of Peace:
  consensus rule through fifty sachems chosen by clan mothers; longhouse
  villages; a diplomatic power Europeans had to bargain with.
- **PLAINS AND SOUTHWEST (comparison)** — Different environments encouraged
  different strategies: scarce-water farming towns vs. mixed farming, hunting,
  and trade. Environment influenced without mechanically determining.

## At stake

**Regional diversity** — North America in 1491 was not one society waiting to
be discovered. Start with diversity, not a single stereotype.

## Outcome

For an APUSH argument, connect environment to evidence: geography shaped
available resources, communities made choices about agriculture, settlement,
and exchange, and those choices produced regional diversity. Treat Indigenous
peoples as active societies, not passive actors.

None. All narration is host voice; no speeches are dramatized in this lesson.
