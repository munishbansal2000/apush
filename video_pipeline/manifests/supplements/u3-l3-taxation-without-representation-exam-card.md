# Exam Card — Taxation Without Representation (1763–1774)

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The two doctrines

- **VIRTUAL REPRESENTATION (Parliament)** — Every member of Parliament
  represents the whole empire, including colonists who never voted. Most
  Britons couldn't vote either. Therefore Parliament can tax the colonies.
- **ACTUAL REPRESENTATION (colonists)** — Only a legislature the colonists
  themselves elected — their own assemblies — can tax them. A distant
  Parliament taxing them without consent violates the rights of Englishmen.

## At stake

**Who can tax?** — not how much. The Tea Act of 1773 made tea *cheaper*,
and the colonists destroyed it anyway: buying it meant accepting
Parliament's right to tax them. Principle outweighed price.

## Outcome

No compromise held. Repeal of the Stamp Act (1766) came paired with the
Declaratory Act claiming Parliament's power "in all cases whatsoever."
Each tax → resistance → harsher measure widened the breach: the
**escalation spiral** that runs straight to Lexington and Concord (1775).

## Chain to memorize

War debt → Sugar Act (1764) → Stamp Act (1765) → Townshend Acts (1767) →
Boston Massacre (1770) → Tea Party (1773) → Intolerable Acts (1774) →
First Continental Congress (Sept 1774).
