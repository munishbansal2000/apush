# Exam Card — 1680: The Revolt That Worked

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The chain (positions)

- **I. ACOMA · 1598–99** — Oñate's terror: 800+ killed by cannon, survivors
  maimed (one foot for men over 25) and enslaved. Eighty years of quiet —
  not loyalty.
- **II. THE CAUSES** — Mission labor in the fields, 1670s drought, priests
  punishing Pueblo ceremonies as witchcraft. In 1675 the governor arrests 47
  religious leaders; 3 are hanged.
- **III. POPÉ'S PLAN** — From Taos, Popé unites the villages with knotted
  cords: one knot untied each morning, runners carrying the countdown. On
  August 10, 1680, every village rises at once.
- **IV. THE RESULT** — 300+ Spaniards dead, 21 of 33 priests, churches burned,
  Santa Fe besieged, survivors flee to El Paso. Twelve years of freedom.

## At stake

**AP Topic 2.5** — the only Native revolt that threw the colonizers out
entirely, and what it proved: Indigenous people could defeat a European
empire, and the empire had to rule differently afterward.

## Outcome

Spain returns in 1692 changed: a defender for Pueblo rights, land grants for
Pueblo families, and an end to crushing the old religion. Accommodation —
not the British answer of removal.

## Dramatization note

Oñate's sentence dramatizes the Spanish trial's punishments; Popé's speech
dramatizes his documented program (drive out the Spanish, destroy churches,
ban Christian names, return to the old ways) from Pueblo accounts including
the 1681 Josephe testimony.
