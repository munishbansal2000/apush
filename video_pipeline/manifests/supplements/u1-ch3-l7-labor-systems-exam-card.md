# Exam Card — From Encomienda to Slavery

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The chain (positions)

- **I. THE GRANT** — Encomienda: the Crown grants a colonist the labor (not
  the land) of a Native community, justified as Christianization and
  "protection." In practice: mines, fields, tribute.
- **II. THE COLLAPSE** — Disease and overwork destroy the workforce; by one
  Spanish count only a few hundred Taíno are left on Hispaniola within
  decades. The Crown replaces encomienda with repartimiento (1549) — a
  draft, still forced.
- **III. THE REPLACEMENT** — Planters turn to enslaved African labor: for
  life, hereditary, and legally property — a different kind of bondage than
  anything before it. Sugar makes it profitable at scale.
- **IV. THE LADDER** — The casta system ranks colonial society by ancestry:
  peninsulares at the top, Africans and Natives at the bottom. Rigid in
  theory; in practice, people moved, mixed, and bought their way up.

## At stake

**AP Topic 1.5** — how the colonies solved their labor problem, and what
that solution built: a society organized by race.

## Outcome

By the late 1500s the labor system has shifted from Indigenous tribute to
African slavery, and the casta hierarchy orders the colonial world that
results.

## Dramatization note

The Las Casas voice is adapted from *A Short Account of the Destruction of
the Indies* (written 1542, published 1552). The Taíno population figure
("a few hundred") follows the Spanish count cited in the lesson.
