# Exam Card — Atlantic Contact and the Columbian Exchange

*8–10 second silent hold. Designed to be paused/screenshotted. Also shipped
as a static image (NLE).*

## The chain (positions)

- **I. MOTIVES** — wealth, rivalry, faith; Tordesillas 1494 splits the world.
- **II. EXCHANGE** — crops, animals, diseases, people, ideas crossed both
  directions.
- **III. COST** — 50–90% died of disease; survivors faced the encomienda.
- **IV. VERDICT** — an Atlantic World, connected but never equal.

## At stake

**AP Topics 1.3, 1.4** — why contact transformed three continents so
unevenly: who paid, who profited.

## Outcome

By the mid-1500s the Atlantic is a single connected system — of commerce,
conquest, and coerced labor — whose costs fall hardest on the Americas.

## Dramatization note

The Columbus journal reading is adapted from his October 12, 1492 entry,
not verbatim. The Four Voyages map (1906) and de Bry landing engraving
(c. 1594) are period illustrations, honestly dated on screen.
